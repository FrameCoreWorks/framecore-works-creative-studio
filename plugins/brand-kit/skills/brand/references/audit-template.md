# brand-audit.md outline

Write in the user's language. Every observation names its source (page, selector, screenshot, quoted line) or says
`Unknown`. Keep it to what helps a decision; a short audit with sources beats a long one without.

```markdown
# Brand audit: {Brand name}

Source: {URL}, {pages read}/{pages tried} pages, {date}, method static HTML/CSS {+ screenshots desktop/mobile}.

## Identity in one line
{What the company is, for whom, why choose it — as the site itself says it, quoted.}

## Visual system
- Palette: {hex, role, where used} (CSS values, not pixel samples)
- Typography: {headline family, body family, how they are served} (licences Unknown)
- Logo: {file URL, variants found, what is missing: white version, mark, clear space}
- Imagery: {photo or illustration, people or product, light, colour treatment} (from screenshots)
- Layout and motion: {density, grid, buttons, motion if any}

## Voice and messaging
- Address: {Ty / Państwo / you, formal or informal} with a quoted example
- Tone: {three adjectives}, each with one quoted line
- Uses: {recurring words and phrases}
- Avoids: {what never appears, or what the brand should avoid, marked as a proposal}
- Promises: {value propositions the site repeats}; claims are observed wording, not approved
- CTAs: {exact texts and their style: imperative, soft, urgent}

## Consistency and gaps
{Contradictions between pages, missing assets, weak points for ads or a new site; each one line, as findings.}

## Unknown and to confirm
{Fields the user must supply or approve: licences, legal lines, banned claims, logo files.}

## Next directions
{The numbered menu from directions.md, with one recommended option and why.}
```

## Voice profile questions

Answer each from quoted copy; leave it open when the sample does not show it.

1. Who speaks: the company ("we"), a person, or nobody (impersonal)?
2. How does it address the reader, and does that change between pages?
3. What does it promise most often, and is any promise backed by a number or proof on the page?
4. Which emotions does it aim for: trust, appetite, ambition, calm, urgency?
5. How long are its sentences, and does it use questions, lists, humour, jargon?
6. What would sound wrong in this brand's mouth?
