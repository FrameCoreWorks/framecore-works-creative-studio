# UGC formats, scripts and disclosure

Checked 2026-10-08. Speaker types, formats and script structure are stable craft. Platform specifications and disclosure rules change; each carries its source and check date. A client's, platform's or market's current requirement wins. This reference is a production starting point, not legal advice; send a legal question about a specific campaign to the user's counsel.

Hook mechanisms, retention and capture realism are in [video craft: UGC, short form and original hooks](../../video-prompt-architect/references/video-craft.md#18-ugc-short-form-and-original-hooks); this file does not repeat them.

## 1. Decide who is speaking first

The speaker type decides what the script may say and what must be disclosed.

| Speaker | May say | Must not imply | Disclosure |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Real customer, testimony supplied with consent | Their own words and experience; edits approved by them | Results beyond what they reported; typicality without evidence | The incentive, if any (free product, payment, discount) |
| Paid or gifted creator | Their honest use and opinion | Long-term results from a first use; claims the brand cannot substantiate | The relationship, in the content and with the platform label |
| Employee or founder | The brand's own view | Being an ordinary customer | The connection, in the content |
| Actor | Scripted lines in a dramatisation | Actual use, ownership or results as personal fact | That it is a dramatisation when it could pass for a real customer |
| Synthetic presenter or cloned voice | Scripted, clearly fictional lines | Being a real person, a customer or someone who used the product | That it is AI-generated wherever it could be taken as real (see section 5) |

A synthetic presenter is never presented as a customer, and a creator-style look does not establish purchase, use, results or endorsement. Fabricated reviews and testimonials stay forbidden in every speaker type.

## 2. Formats

| Format | Structure | Proof it needs | Watch for |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Talking head | Hook, personal context, product moment, observed result, CTA | The speaker's real experience or a labelled dramatisation | Reading tone; one claim per sentence |
| Demo or how-to | Outcome first, steps in real time, result on screen | The action visibly working in one take or honest cuts | Speed-ups and cuts that hide failure |
| Unboxing or first impression | Package, first touch, first use, honest reaction | Only what a first use can show | Durability or long-term claims |
| Problem and solution | Relatable friction, failed attempt, product as the turn, visible change | The change shown, not narrated | Exaggerated problem acting |
| Comparison | Same task, same conditions, side by side | Fair, repeatable conditions; substantiation for comparative claims | Rigged conditions; named competitors |
| Objection answer | One objection quoted, answered with proof | Evidence for the answer | Several objections in one video |
| Before and after | Same angle, light and time gap stated | Real, unretouched, permitted material | Synthetic or retouched "after"; health and cosmetic claims |
| Routine or day in the life | Product inside a real routine | Plausible, unforced use | Product overload |
| Reaction, stitch or duet | Response to another post | Rights and the platform's rules for the original | Misquoting the original |

## 3. Script skeleton

A planning heuristic for paid short-form UGC, not a platform rule. Adjust to the brief and the speaker's natural pace.

| Part | Job | 15 s | 30 s | 60 s |
| --- | --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Hook | One lead mechanism, legible without sound | 0 to 2 s | 0 to 3 s | 0 to 3 s |
| Context | Why this person speaks now | 2 to 4 s | 3 to 8 s | 3 to 12 s |
| Proof | Demo, observed result or evidence | 4 to 11 s | 8 to 22 s | 12 to 45 s |
| Objection or detail | One hesitation answered | optional | 22 to 26 s | 45 to 53 s |
| CTA | One clear action, matching the offer | 11 to 15 s | 26 to 30 s | 53 to 60 s |

Write for speech: short sentences, one claim each, numbers only with a source. Sound-off viewing needs the hook and the proof in picture or captions; use [Caption Studio](../../caption-studio/SKILL.md) for caption timing.

## 5. Disclosure

Disclose in two places: inside the content itself and with the platform's own label. Neither replaces the other.

### Platform labels

| Platform | Commercial relationship | AI-generated media |
| --- | --- | --- |
| TikTok | Commercial content disclosure setting; branded content for a third party shows a paid-partnership label (TikTok help, read through a search summary on 2026-10-08) | In TikTok Ads Manager, a disclaimer is mandatory for "AI-generated, synthetic, or manipulated media", meaning media that is completely AI-generated or real material significantly modified by AI ([TikTok Ads help](https://ads.tiktok.com/help/article/about-ad-disclaimers-in-tiktok-ads-manager), page dated September 2025). For organic posts, TikTok's own pages did not load; search summaries say realistic AI-generated images, audio and video must be labelled |
| Meta (Facebook, Instagram) | The Paid partnership label is required for branded content, including gifted products ([Meta Business Help](https://www.facebook.com/business/help/213764212711862); the page did not load on 2026-10-08, so this comes from a search summary) | Check the current AI labelling rules in the app |
| YouTube | Tick the paid promotion option in YouTube Studio; viewers see a disclosure at the start (search summary of YouTube Help) | Disclose realistic AI content that makes a real person appear to say or do something they did not, alters footage of a real event or place, or generates a realistic scene that did not occur; non-realistic content, aesthetic edits and cloning one's own voice for voice-over are exempt ([YouTube Help](https://support.google.com/youtube/answer/14328491?hl=en), read 2026-10-08) |

### Law: starting points

- **Poland.** The UOKiK President's recommendations on labelling influencer advertising (press release of 26 September 2022, [archive](https://archiwum.uokik.gov.pl/aktualnosci.php?news_id=18898)): label clearly at first glance, on mobile and desktop, in the profile's language, and name the brand. Recommended terms include `#reklama` or `[REKLAMA]`, `#materiałreklamowy` or `[MATERIAŁ REKLAMOWY]`, `#współpracareklamowa` or `[WSPÓŁPRACA REKLAMOWA]`, "Płatna współpraca z marką XYZ" and "Reklama marki XYZ"; `AUTOPROMOCJA` for one's own products; `PREZENT` only for a first PR parcel, later parcels from the same brand count as advertising. Use the platform label as well (two-level labelling). The press release states fines of up to 10% of turnover.
- **European Union.** The Unfair Commercial Practices Directive (2005/29/EC) blacklists paid editorial content not identified as advertising and falsely posing as a consumer; since the 2019 amendment (Directive (EU) 2019/2161) it also blacklists submitting or commissioning fake consumer reviews or endorsements. The AI Act (Regulation (EU) 2024/1689), Article 50(4): deployers of an AI system that generates or manipulates image, audio or video content constituting a deep fake "shall disclose that the content has been artificially generated or manipulated"; for an evidently artistic, creative, satirical or fictional work, disclosure may be limited to a manner that does not hamper the work. Article 50(5) requires the information to be clear and distinguishable at the latest at first exposure. The obligation applies from 2 August 2026. Secondary sources read on 2026-10-08 report that the 2026 amendments did not postpone Article 50(4); the amending text was not checked.
- **United States.** The FTC's [Disclosures 101 for Social Media Influencers](https://www.ftc.gov/business-guidance/resources/disclosures-101-social-media-influencers) (November 2019): disclose any financial, employment, personal or family relationship; put the disclosure with the endorsement, in the video itself and not only in its description, and repeat it during live streams; "advertisement", "ad" and "sponsored" are clear, while "sp", "spon", "collab", "thanks" or "ambassador" alone are not; a platform's tool may not be enough on its own. The Endorsement Guides (16 CFR Part 255) were revised in 2023. The FTC's [rule on consumer reviews and testimonials](https://www.ftc.gov/news-events/news/press-releases/2024/08/federal-trade-commission-announces-final-rule-banning-fake-reviews-testimonials) (16 CFR Part 465, announced 14 August 2024, in force since October 2024) prohibits fake reviews and testimonials, including AI-generated ones and those from people without real experience of the product, and allows civil penalties for knowing violations.

For another market, research its current rules before writing a disclosure line; until then the requirement is Unknown.

## 6. Handoff checklist

- Speaker type and what they may claim.
- Format and script skeleton with timings.
- Claim IDs with their evidence, and the claims withheld.
- Disclosure lines for the content and the platform label, in the market's language.
- AI disclosure when a synthetic presenter, cloned voice or altered footage is used.
- Platform specification and its check date.
