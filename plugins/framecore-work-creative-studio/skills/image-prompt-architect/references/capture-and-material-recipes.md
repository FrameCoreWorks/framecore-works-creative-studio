## 1. Start with what must be visible together

Choose the asset's decisive relationship, then the view that proves it. A
portrait needs the relevant identity cues; a garment comparison may need the
closure and collar together; a product-use frame may need contact and the
label together. If those features cannot be legible at the requested scale,
change the authorized composition or propose separate coverage. Do not hide
the conflict with indiscriminate blur or a promise of microscopic detail.

Use a short capture sentence before writing a long prompt:

- **Evidence:** what the viewer must recognize or inspect.
- **View:** frame size, viewpoint and perspective behavior that expose it.
- **Focus:** features that should resolve together; what may fall away.
- **Light/material:** which visible response gives the surface its character.
- **Exclusion:** one competing feature or false implication to suppress.

A coherent light system may contain several motivated sources. “One system”
does not mean that every image must have one lamp, one reflection or symmetric
catchlights. Distinguish deliberately visible studio reflections from artifacts.

## 2. Identity portrait with readable clothing

**Use:** restrained chest-up portrait in which the person and upper garment
must both remain recognizable. **Avoid as default:** beauty close-up, dramatic
wide perspective, or a crop chosen only because it resembles an influencer ad.

**Construction:** choose a camera relation that does not unintentionally make
a nearer facial feature dominate. Keep the shoulders sufficiently readable to
explain the collar. Place the person within a simple field whose brightness
supports the intended first read. Describe an eye/face focus priority while
retaining enough useful garment structure for the stated job. A broad lateral
key and restrained fill are one possible treatment when lighting is open, not
a prescription over a locked source.

**Material decisions:** skin is allowed natural variation without invented
marks; hair follows existing masses and parting; fabric gets construction-led
folds rather than identical grain everywhere. Restrained grade must not turn
the garment into another protected color variant.

**Failure fork:** wrong face geometry goes back to authority/view/structure;
correct face with smeared eyes calls for a focus/detail correction; a correct
portrait with an unreadable required closure needs a framing/focus decision.
Changing skin texture cannot solve the first or third problem.

**Check:** compare the declared identity anchors and visible garment junctions
against their proper sources. Focus instructions are not evidence that a future
render is sharp; inspect actual output at the intended use size and, where
available, enough resolution to resolve the questioned feature.

## 3. Clear glass with an opaque closure or label

**Use:** product frame that must preserve the shape of a transparent body and
separately explain opaque parts. Source truth determines tint, fill and label;
lighting direction alone cannot establish them.

**Construction:** decide what should be seen through the glass and what should
be reflected by it. Keep the background arrangement simple enough that
transmission can be understood. Give the opaque closure and any label a clear
reading territory; do not let a decorative highlight become a substitute for
the product's true edge or obscure mandatory lettering. Specify a supported
resting plane and coherent contact.

**Trade-off:** a darker outline can reveal a clear edge, yet an excessively
thick uniform contour may make the product look painted. A broad bright region
can describe a curved surface, yet it must not flatten away the label. The
choice depends on the actual object, viewing scale and approved art direction.

**Failure fork:** if the bottle geometry is wrong, return to product authority.
If geometry is sound but a white streak remains from the old setting, test the
authorized reflection change. If the user locked that streak pixel-for-pixel,
resolve the conflict before writing the edit. If a transparent area contains
an impossible fragment of the old background, repair transmission within the
agreed scope; do not recolor the glass to hide it.

**Check:** inspect body/closure junction, supported label, edge continuity,
transmission, contact and any newly implied contents separately. The complete
[product edit example](../assets/reference-transfer-worked-examples.md#example-b-transparent-product-with-a-new-support)
shows one bounded application without a native mask or optical-precision claim.

## 4. Matte fabric with one meaningful physical action

**Use:** a source-supported sleeve, strap or garment interacting with a body.
Start with an observable action such as resting a hand on a table or carrying
an approved bag. Define ownership and support; keep the action compatible with
the garment's visible construction. Do not add a pose change during a
texture-only correction.

**Construction:** distinguish three scales of information: silhouette and fit;
seams, folds and closures; surface texture. The first two should survive at the
actual viewing size. If the seam is lost in shadow, consider a permitted local
lighting or viewpoint decision before exaggerating thread and weave. For
patterned fabric, protect repeat direction and construction around folds; do
not require equally sharp pattern in every depth plane.

**Failure fork:** an extra seam is a construction defect, not a texture issue.
A sleeve that floats from the wrist is fit/contact. A correct knit becoming
noise only at thumbnail size is a reproduction/viewing problem. These require
different interventions. Use the actual observed mismatch to choose the branch.

## 5. Turn a recipe into a compact prompt

Resolve the construction with the [still shot card](../assets/still-shot-card.md),
then compile ordinary prose. Include only details visible in the requested
frame. For a complex junction, spend words on who touches what, which boundary
stays visible and how support works. Omit equipment prestige, redundant camera
numbers, arbitrary processing stacks and unsupported product attributes.

If a named renderer exposes verified controls, place them in their real control
surface. The semantic prompt should remain understandable without pretending
that lens, focus or lighting prose is calibrated metadata. A ratio phrase is
intended framing until the actual output dimensions have been checked.
