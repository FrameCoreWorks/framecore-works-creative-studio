# Visual languages and material logic

Owner: commercial-visual-campaign-director. Version: 0.1.0-dev.2. Updated: 2026-09-24. Design synthesis adapted from the static-design composition, translation and movements atlases. This is selected craft coverage, not a complete world history or a library of native generator presets.

## Sort the label before using it

A historical language, layout stance, image-construction method and surface effect control different decisions. “Minimal, Swiss, collage, riso” is not four interchangeable styles. Resolve what each contributes and omit anything that has no job.

- A composition stance controls reduction, density or ordering.
- A historical/contemporary language supplies transferable relations among type, image and cultural context.
- An image construction describes how the scene or sign is made.
- A material/process treatment explains visible surface behavior.
- A mood adjective is unresolved until expressed through choices.

One primary language with a compatible treatment is a useful starting point, not a prohibition on deliberate hybrids. Follow a coherent user-specified hybrid when every component has a role. Never promise that a slash label is a generator control.

## Resolve ambiguous or mixed labels before committing

Treat a style word as evidence about the requested direction, then decide whether its meaning is already clear from the brief. Separate historical or contemporary visual languages, composition stances, image-construction methods, material/process treatments, type treatments, and mood words before combining them. A list is not a finished art direction.

- If the context resolves a broad label, or the user explicitly invites a provisional choice, state one brief-specific interpretation as reversible. Explain the visible choices that make it useful, and keep the interpretation tentative; it is not user approval, a project lock, a verified brand attribute, or a generator preset. Do not ask a ceremonial question when the user has already authorized a low-risk working assumption.
- If the label could materially change the concept, cultural or historical reference, message-image relation, or reading hierarchy, and the brief does not resolve the choice, ask one concise question about that decision. Do not deliver a final direction or prompt that depends on the unanswered choice. Do not restart intake or turn one ambiguity into a questionnaire.
- If the user asks for a provisional concept before choosing a decade or other style detail, deliver the concept at that stage. Keep the undecided historical treatment explicitly open; withhold only the period-specific final direction or prompt that actually depends on it. A reversible concept proposal is not an approved decade choice.
- Preserve a user-requested hybrid when its components have distinct, compatible jobs. Name each job in ordinary design terms—for example, a grid may organize title and event facts while a linocut-like treatment shapes the central image. Check that their combination still serves the communication goal and legibility. Do not reject a coherent hybrid merely because its labels come from different classes.
- If the user asks for a stack with no roles or an unresolved hierarchy, do not flatten every label into an effect pile, silently discard parts, or declare the categories inherently incompatible. Ask one organizing question that distinguishes a deliberate visible collision from a coordinated system with assigned component roles. Until that material decision is resolved, withhold the final direction or prompt.

After the label decision is clear, continue through the existing brief, copy, reference, and static anti-slop checks. This is a decision rule within this owner, not an additional gate, router, style blacklist, numerical materiality threshold, or fixed maximum number of labels. Keep user-approved constraints intact and make only the assumption reversible; do not turn it into an approval or lock without the user's choice.

## Historical-language decision cards

These cards describe practical attributes, not instructions to copy a particular artwork.

| Language | Transferable design decision | Appropriate question | Avoid |
|---|---|---|---|
| Art Nouveau-informed | Integrate contour, lettering and a rhythmic enclosing field | Does an enveloping organic rhythm help this subject? | A generic ornamental woman and border |
| Art Deco-informed | Stepped geometry, streamlined mass and controlled axial tension | Is speed, arrival, ceremony or modernity central? | Automatic black-and-gold luxury |
| Object poster / Sachplakat-informed | Compress recognition into one actual object and its name | Can the product itself make the argument? | “Minimal” staging with no product truth |
| Constructivist-informed | Directional force, scale collision and assembled graphic argument | What does the diagonal or photo-fragment relationship say? | Red/black propaganda tokens without context |
| New Typography-informed | Order a page as an asymmetric information field | Which arrangement makes information easier to discover? | Confusing it with generic primary-color shapes |
| Bauhaus-informed | Investigate relationships of form, material and function | Why do these elementary forms belong to the task? | A decorative school-name sticker |
| Swiss / International Typographic-informed | Measured grid, clear type/photo relation and disciplined hierarchy | Does the grid improve scanning or a series? | Assuming one famous sans face is the whole method |
| Polish Poster School-informed | Compress interpretation into a metaphor, wit or type-image tension | What new interpretive relation belongs to this work? | Rough paint plus a surreal face as a national stereotype |
| Psychedelic-informed | Use perceptual rhythm and controlled decoding friction | Is slower expressive reading appropriate? | Applying the same distortion to dates and directions |
| Pop/reproduction-informed | Work with scale, repetition and mass-media conventions | What tension exists between this message and a familiar commercial code? | Decorative comic dots everywhere |
| Systemic modernist | Establish repeatable rules with meaningful variation | What remains recognizable when subject and format change? | Every variant becoming the same centered template |
| Authorial contemporary | Define a rule, then purposeful exceptions | Can the user identify the authored decision? | Trend labels replacing an idea |

Regional traditions are not interchangeable. A request relying on Cuban, Indian, Japanese or other histories requires dedicated sources; this selected atlas does not pretend to cover them all. Political symbols, affiliations and documentary claims require appropriate factual and project authority, not assumptions from a moodboard.

## Contemporary labels translated into decisions

| User label | Operational translation | Test |
|---|---|---|
| Minimal | Reduce to a word, sign or relation with protected space | Is the remaining element enough to communicate? |
| Maximal | Deliberate density organized by a stable entry and secondary paths | Can required facts still be found? |
| Editorial | Make type, crop and captioning express an argument | Is there a reading structure, not just a fashion photo? |
| Brutalist | Blunt scale, exposed structure and purposeful rawness | Does the bluntness sharpen the message? |
| Cyberpunk | Specific technological/social tension through infrastructure, light and information | Is there more than neon rain? |
| Y2K | Chosen early-digital optimism, translucent forms or era-specific geometry | Is the period coherent and not an icon dump? |
| Retrofuturist | A defined historical imagination of a future | Which period's future, and why this one? |
| Solarpunk | Concrete repair, community or infrastructure relationships | Are environmental claims absent unless supported? |
| Punk/zine | Urgent authored copy, cut-and-paste hierarchy and reproduction logic | Is it a meaningful voice, not generic distress? |
| Surreal | One improbable but precise relationship | Does the surprise deepen this subject? |
| Glass-like | A local translucent depth plane with protected opaque type | Is essential text still readable? |
| Pixel | Authored grid scale, cluster logic and limited palette | Is it designed as pixels instead of filtered? |
| Clay/sculptural | Tactile simplified volumes, deliberate light and scale | Does cuteness serve rather than replace meaning? |
| Handwritten | A specific expressive gesture in a defined role | Are functional details protected? |
| Retro | A named period, mechanism or reproduction context | Have sepia and scratches been used as a shortcut? |
| Premium | Restraint, precision or material decisions justified by the product | What observable decision makes it appropriate? |

Preserve a supplied style constraint unless it creates a material conflict. Do not refuse ornament, vivid color, chrome or glow categorically. Require a job. A chrome surface may be the actual product; forbidding it to satisfy an anti-slop word list would break source truth.

## Material follows a cause

| Process/look | Visible logic to express | What not to claim |
|---|---|---|
| Lithographic | Drawn mark character and separated color fields | That a generated raster was printed from stones |
| Screenprinted | Spot-color shapes, purposeful overlaps and a clear ink hierarchy | Real separations, trapping or verified ink matching |
| Risograph-like | Stencil/drum-based limited-color logic, optional local registration variation | That blur or misregistration is compulsory |
| Letterpress-like | Relief-derived edges and, if useful, subtle pressure evidence | Deep deboss on every letter or actual press readiness |
| Offset-like | Controlled reproduction, paper interaction and considered screening | That random noise proves a print process |
| Halftone | Tone represented by a deliberate dot structure on the relevant field | That the same dot overlay belongs on every material |
| Photomontage | Sources, crops and scale relationships with semantic roles | Documentary authenticity of an invented composite |
| Collage | Specific edge/material contrasts in one composition | A pile of equally important fragments |
| Woodcut/linocut-like | Positive/negative shape and plausible carved marks | Distress as a universal substitute for carving logic |

Name substrate, ink and overprint separately if a limited-color simulation depends on them. A third apparent color may come from overlap rather than another ink; do not contradict that mechanism with arbitrary accents. Material may be nearly invisible if the task needs a clean sign.

Physical production remains separate. Actual spot inks, profiles, stock, bleed, overprint and proofing require the printer/DTP contract. A simulated style does not establish those properties.

## Applying a language without copying

Describe the transferable construction: a diagonal reading field, one flat object silhouette, type functioning as an obstacle, a repeat rule, a limited-ink relation. Rebuild it around the user's own subject and copy. Do not combine the same image, headline placement, palette, distress and secondary symbols from a reference and call the result an original direction.

For a named creator or work, distinguish requested analysis, broad influence and a near-replica of protected composition. Preserve lawful creative help while making a new, project-specific arrangement. Do not invent rights clearance or attribution.
