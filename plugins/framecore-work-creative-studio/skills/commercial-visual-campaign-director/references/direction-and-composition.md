# Authorial static direction and composition

Owner: commercial-visual-campaign-director. Version: 0.1.0-dev.1. Updated: 2026-09-21. Stable craft synthesis from static-design concept/composition/production references, Poster Creator ROOT/META and AI-First Creative Director K01–K04. This is an authored working method, not a universal design law or proof of market effectiveness.

## 1. Discover the actual communication problem

A poster occupies a viewing situation. Ask what must be understood there, not merely what aesthetic the client likes. A visitor deciding where to queue, a commuter glancing at an event announcement and a reader examining a theatre poster have different jobs. “Premium”, “cinematic” and “modern” name an ambition, not a message.

Extract from the brief:

- the subject and verified distinguishing fact;
- the intended response: recognize, attend, understand, compare, remember, act;
- the likely viewing mode: glance, scan or deliberate reading;
- necessary information and its consequence if missed;
- what the user values in the initial idea or reference;
- practical constraints and decisions still open.

The missing information may be a conceptual choice, not a field in a form. “Czy chodzi o zaproszenie nowych osób, czy o informację dla zapisanych?” can change an event poster more than a question about a preferred font. Conversely, a complete directed brief needs no new audience interview.

Separate source facts from hypotheses. An audience observation supplied by a client is useful, but not a measured market insight. A fictional event used to demonstrate a layout stays fictional. Do not improve persuasiveness by inventing the lowest price, a certification, a waiting list, a testimonial or an environmental saving.

Distinguish visual distinctiveness from a factual unique selling proposition. An original composition can use a supported project detail or an openly fictional metaphor without claiming that the offer is objectively unique. Do not block a truthful creative direction solely because no exclusive market advantage was supplied; do not invent one to satisfy a distinctiveness check.

## 2. Find specific material

Look for a physical behavior, habit, place, constraint, contradiction, phrase, consequence or object relation already belonging to the project. It should change the image, not just the caption.

For a repair workshop, relevant material might be a repeatedly mended handle, a workbench organized around the next person's object, or the difference between discarding and giving time. A generic glowing lightbulb does not become specific because the event title is placed below it.

When the user mentions a personal association, explore what is compelling about it: action, emotional distance, texture, memory of a place, uncomfortable contrast. Do not turn that association into a fabricated autobiographical narrative. If the user has no association, offer invented observations as options they can reject.

## 3. Select a mechanism rather than an effect

| Mechanism | What can carry the message | Useful test | Frequent misuse |
|---|---|---|---|
| Demonstration | An actual feature becomes visibly useful | Is the benefit shown without unsupported performance claims? | Fake before/after proof |
| Consequence | The result is more telling than the action | Is the causal connection understandable? | An unrelated dramatic image |
| Absence | A missing expected element creates meaning | Would the viewer know what is missing? | Empty space that says nothing |
| Reversal | Familiar roles change | Does reversal expose this project's tension? | Novelty for its own sake |
| Scale/context shift | One relationship becomes newly legible | Is the disproportion necessary to the idea? | Generic enormous product in a city |
| Type as event | Spacing, repetition, interruption or form performs meaning | Does the word/form relation carry the thesis? | Decorative distortion that destroys required text |
| Evidence field | Clear juxtaposition or organized data persuades | Is every item sourced and comparable? | Invented statistics or pseudo-data |
| Narrative window | A crop or scene suggests a particular world | What does this point of view allow the viewer to infer? | Stock people performing a generic emotion |
| Information beacon | One datum organizes the whole | Is this the datum the viewer needs first? | Date made large merely to fill space |

These are prompts for judgment, not a checklist to exhaust. Direct information can be the strongest concept. A fire-exit notice need not be a riddle. An exact logo-color repair does not require any new mechanism.

## 4. Develop genuinely different routes

When the concept is unresolved and exploration is requested, normally show three routes, unless the user requests another count. A route includes:

1. its concrete thesis and visual event;
2. the proposed or locked wording and how it contributes;
3. the order of attention;
4. composition and type/image relationship;
5. medium, material and color logic;
6. a relevant benefit, tradeoff and unresolved factual risk.

Different font/palette choices around the same centered product are variants, not different directions. Compare different arguments or audience entry points. A story of repeated use, a demonstration of repair and a direct invitation to bring a broken object are three mechanisms. Blue, red and green versions of one wrench are not.

Do not make exploration a barrier after the user has selected a mechanism. Explore layout within the accepted concept if that is the request. If two candidate routes are combined and wording or meaning materially changes, present the new combined candidate for selection rather than pretending it inherits both approvals.

## 5. Construct a reading architecture

Start with attention, not coordinates. Relative size, contrast, position, isolation, faces, saturated colors and directional marks all affect salience. The largest text does not necessarily win if a bright face or flare occupies the main field.

Separate attention role from requiredness:

- must-read: the meaning or action that cannot be missed;
- should-read: information discovered after recognition;
- metadata: credits, conditions or practical details that may still be mandatory;
- decoration: not semantic information.

Mandatory metadata is never disposable. A one-word poster may have one reading level. A program may need a longer deliberate scan. Do not force three levels everywhere.

Choose a governing composition:

| Structure | Useful function | What must be specified |
|---|---|---|
| Iconic anchor | Recognize one object, sign or intervention | Dominant position, protected silhouette, supporting text relation |
| Typographic engine | Make the title itself an event | Type behavior, repeat/spacing logic, where functional data lives |
| Editorial field | Organize several facts or a comparison | Alignment, grouping, column relation and scan order |
| Conceptual relation | Make two elements form a single argument | Their scale, overlap or absence, with minimal competing detail |
| Narrative window | Reveal a scene through a purposeful crop | Point of view, what is included/excluded, title relation |
| Kinetic field | Convey pace through repetition/direction | Directional axis, stable reading anchor, controlled density |
| Modular system | Sustain a campaign across subjects/formats | Fixed relation, variable slots, permitted changes |

Centering, symmetry and asymmetry are tools. Centering can establish ritual or a sign; asymmetry can create tension or efficient hierarchy. “Avoid centering” is no more intelligent than “always center the hero”.

## 6. Grid, mass and spatial relations

A grid is an alignment system, not necessarily a visible pattern. Select a few structural edges: title start, image boundary, detail column and safe perimeter. Optical alignment may require adjustment where a rounded letter or diagonal shape appears inset despite numerical alignment.

Describe actual relationships: the title occupies the left upper field; the key image cuts into its lower edge without obscuring letters; the practical information aligns to the title's first character; the CTA is separate from the date. Avoid “balanced, harmonious layout” without saying how.

Whitespace can protect a phrase, separate groups, imply absence or increase perceived scale. Its amount should follow the task. Dense maximalist work can be excellent when it has a stable entry and navigable substructure; minimal work can fail because its lone image is generic.

Check mass through silhouette and reduction. If the main event disappears when the asset is reduced, the idea may depend on details the audience will not see. A dark lower block can balance a top-heavy headline, but should still have a communication role. Do not add a shape solely because a corner looks empty.

## 7. Type and image as collaborators

Choose their relationship before a font category:

- Type leads; image acts as evidence, interruption or field.
- Image leads; type names, reframes or directs action.
- Type and image are mutually dependent, as in a letterform becoming part of the mechanism.
- Information itself leads; illustration may be unnecessary.

Avoid redundant description. If an image unmistakably shows a repair, a support line that merely says “repairing objects” adds little. It might instead clarify that visitors bring one item. Conversely, a clever headline may require plain support to prevent misunderstanding.

Typography is covered deeply in typography.md. Refer to visible behavior, not a promise that a raster generator has a particular installed font. Exact font licensing and editable type belong to production, not a prompt adjective.

## 8. Color and material serve the structure

Assign palette roles: base field, dominant information, functional support, one meaningful accent if needed. A branded color can be protected without every surface being that color. Check actual local contrast, not the palette swatch in isolation.

Explain what produces material appearance. A limited-ink simulation can use broad opaque shapes and a deliberate overlap. It does not require dirt, torn edges or registration errors. Photographic grain belongs to the photographic field, not automatically to small type. Clean vector-like shapes remain raster unless delivered as paths.

Do not confuse historical language with process or surface. A Swiss-informed grid can be simulated with screenprint material, but each decision needs a role. A requested hybrid is allowed when the hierarchy survives; there is no universal numerical ban on style ingredients.

## 9. Authorial and anti-slop pre-prompt gate

This is the blocking review owned by the static direction stage, between a proposed or selected direction and the image prompt compiler. It applies whenever the task creates or materially revises a static creative direction. An exact local edit or execution-only handoff may inherit an already accepted direction if the concept, copy, format and protected properties remain unchanged. A material change reopens only affected checks and their dependencies.

Do not replace this review with a style blacklist, “human design” adjective, numeric score or longer negative prompt. Judge the direction in its actual communication context. For each relevant criterion, determine whether it passes, fails or is not applicable; a non-applicable criterion is not a failure and should have a brief reason when that distinction affects the decision.

| Criterion | Passing evidence |
|---|---|
| Communication goal | The direction can state what the viewer should understand or do in one sentence. |
| Visual thesis | One specific visual or typographic event carries the goal; the hero is not merely an attractive backdrop. |
| Attention | The necessary first read and subsequent scan are intentional for this format and viewing distance. |
| Required information | Required elements have a feasible place in the composition, including designs with no image hero. |
| Style construction | The chosen visual language changes relevant composition, type, colour or material decisions, not just an effect stack. |
| Element roles | Each decorative-looking element has a real communication, hierarchy, navigation, recognition or material-evidence job. |
| Word/form specificity | The complete visible wording and its form belong to this brief. Apply the substitution test to the whole word/form relationship; never delete a type-led title and then demand a separate image concept. |
| Generic effects | Smoke, particles, chrome, neon glow, cityscape, crowds, lens flare, pseudo-3D objects, random grids or arbitrary paper texture appear only when their role is justified. These are diagnostic examples, not categorical bans. |
| Priority versus requiredness | Attention roles and mandatory status are distinct; all required copy/metadata remains readable and decoration never impersonates information. |
| Process/material logic | A print or material look follows an intentional process/material rationale rather than generic damage or vintage treatment. |

Keep source truth, protected brand/product/reference properties, exact copy and format feasibility intact. Existing copy/reference gates remain authoritative; this gate does not replace them.

### Gate outcomes and repair loop

- **PASS:** every applicable criterion passes and no material concept, required-string, source-property or hierarchy failure remains. Hand the direction to the prompt compiler with its accepted thesis, copy status, format, element roles and observable checks. PASS means direction-ready only, not a successful render.
- **REWORK:** any material criterion fails. Withhold the final prompt. Name the failed criterion and its observable evidence, route the correction to the right layer, preserve already accepted decisions, and set one concrete acceptance test. Revise the direction and recheck the failed criteria, dependencies and overall gate before handoff.
- **BLOCKED:** a fact or decision essential to a truthful, specific direction is unavailable. Do not invent it or disguise the gap with generic effects. Ask one concise, material question; resume the gate after the answer.

Choose the upstream correction: a generic or substitutable concept returns to the communication mechanism; a broken word/form relation returns to concept construction; a hierarchy failure changes the reading architecture; an unjustified support element is removed or assigned a defensible job. Do not turn a concept failure into a prompt-wording patch or a render-level repair.

For a repeated repair of the same root cause, preserve passed criteria and revise one primary issue at a time. After two unsuccessful corrections of the same cause, stop cycling and reframe the concept or request the missing decision. Do not emit, label or imply a final generator prompt while the outcome is REWORK or BLOCKED. User-facing explanation should be concise and actionable; do not expose an internal score or checklist dump unless requested.

Deliberate maximalism, vivid colour, centering, texture, ornament, chrome, glow and typographic-only composition can pass when they serve the brief and the reading hierarchy holds. Do not punish the absence of an image hero in a type-led concept; evaluate the full word/form event. A local text error in an actual render belongs to the post-render repair route, not this pre-prompt gate.

## 10. Handoff

Carry the selected thesis, first read, copy status, source roles, format, type/image relation, material rules, adaptations allowed and observable checks. A final direction must be sufficiently concrete for the prompt compiler without asking it to redesign the campaign.

Brief notes may remain concise. Depth means that decisions are grounded and executable, not that every answer exposes this manual.
