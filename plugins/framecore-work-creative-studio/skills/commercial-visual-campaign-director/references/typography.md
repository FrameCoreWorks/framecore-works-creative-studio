# Typography: selection, hierarchy and reading

Owner: commercial-visual-campaign-director. Version: 0.1.0-dev.1. Updated: 2026-09-21. Stable craft from Poster Creator K04 and static-design typography reference. The methods below guide design; exact font availability, file licensing and measurements require separate evidence.

## Start from reading, not a fashionable font

Identify the role: display expression, explanation, navigation, data or a protected brand mark. Establish the language, viewing context, text length and production intent. The same face can work in a large title and fail in tiny reversed type. A low-contrast hairline that looks elegant on a large monitor may disappear on a small social placement.

A typeface is a designed family; a font may denote a particular style or file. A named family in a raster prompt expresses a target, not proof of installation, correct glyph outlines, a licensed file or embedded type. When exact typography is contractually necessary, specify a suitable typesetting handoff.

## Anatomy as a decision tool

| Property | What changes visually | Useful attention |
|---|---|---|
| x-height | Relative height of lowercase bodies | Perceived reading size and density, not an automatic quality ranking |
| Apertures | Openness of c/e/a-like shapes | Distinguishability at small size or adverse contrast |
| Counters | Internal spaces | Avoid filled-in small letters or overly heavy treatments |
| Stroke contrast | Difference between thick/thin strokes | Display elegance versus fragile thin detail |
| Width | Condensed to expanded proportions | Fit and tone; extreme compression can harm long-text reading |
| Terminals/serifs | How strokes end | Character and rhythm; not a guarantee of genre |
| Ascenders/descenders | Vertical extensions | Leading, collisions and word shapes |
| Weight | Stroke mass | Hierarchy and contrast; heavy letters can close counters |
| Optical size | Shapes adjusted for intended size | Prefer suitable text/display variants when actually typesetting |
| Figures | Proportional/tabular, lining/oldstyle | Align prices or schedules without arbitrary spacing |

Choose through consequences: a date block needs clear numerals; a long title may need moderate width, fewer breaks or a redesigned territory. Do not solve every fit issue with an ultra-condensed face.

## Family categories and their uses

Categories overlap. Use them to describe behavior and make a comparison, not to classify every font dogmatically.

| Category | Typical visible qualities | Appropriate use | Failure risk |
|---|---|---|---|
| Humanist sans | Open forms, varied rhythm, calligraphic influence | Accessible information, approachable institutional voice | Treating “friendly” as automatic for every family |
| Grotesque/neo-grotesque sans | Economical, relatively even texture | Editorial hierarchy, modern systems | Generic default with no compositional decision |
| Geometric sans | Repeated geometric relationships | Strong signs, concise titles, geometric systems | Similar letter shapes or rigid long-text rhythm |
| Oldstyle serif | Moderate contrast, organic modulation | Literary/editorial reading, warmer formality | Small reproduction closing delicate details |
| Transitional serif | More vertical stress and sharper contrast | Editorial distinction, controlled formality | Mixing adjacent families with no meaningful difference |
| Modern/high-contrast serif | Strong thick/thin contrast, sharp detail | Large display, editorial drama | Hairlines disappearing at use size |
| Slab serif | Robust serif mass | Forceful titles, industrial or vernacular influence | Heavy blocks crowding dense information |
| Display/experimental | Distinctive structure designed for impact | One expressive role | Applying it to all dates, addresses and terms |
| Script/calligraphic | Connected or gesture-led rhythm | Invitation, signature-like display, selective expression | False authenticity, poor short-word recognition |
| Handwritten/marker | Irregular authored gesture | Informal display or meaningful annotation | Tiny factual information becomes ambiguous |
| Monospace | Repeated cell widths | Deliberate technical register or aligned samples | Cliché “tech look”; not a substitute for a real table |
| Blackletter-derived | Dense historic stroke rhythm | Deliberately chosen cultural/display context | Unintended cultural association and illegibility |
| Stencil/inline/outline | Constructed or interrupted strokes | Short, large statements with a specific material role | Broken counters, missing diacritics and weak small text |

No category is inherently professional or unprofessional. Professional quality comes from fit, spacing, rhythm, contrast and consistency with the message.

## Pair by role and contrast

A practical starting point is one display voice and one functional voice, or a single flexible family. Add another only for an actual distinction, such as a different script or a documented brand requirement.

Useful contrast may be width, weight, structure or rhythm. A condensed display with a spacious humanist functional layer creates different jobs. Two almost identical sans families can create accidental inconsistency. Conversely, a tightly controlled single-family design can express enough contrast through scale, weight and placement.

Do not assign a font to each information item. Group roles. Name which face-like behavior carries headline, supporting line and factual details. For bilingual work, harmonize apparent size and weight across scripts instead of assuming equal point size looks equal.

## Hierarchy is relational

Use scale, weight, contrast, position, alignment and separation together. A quieter detail block can remain readable without competing. Oversized text is not automatically the first read if an adjacent illustration has stronger contrast or a face.

Check a reduction or thumbnail for overall attention, and actual use size for functional reading. Required metadata is allowed to be secondary, not invisible. The first impression may be expressive while the second read is highly functional.

Do not promise universal point sizes or viewing distances from a prompt. Physical readability depends on size, distance, reproduction, lighting, eyesight and the actual letterforms. If the use context is missing and matters, ask for it or label the assumption.

## Line length, leading and line breaks

Line breaks change meaning and rhythm. Keep a semantically connected name, amount/qualifier or date together when feasible. Avoid an accidental isolated final word unless the emphasis is intentional. A headline split should support the concept and leave sufficient counterspace.

Store exact text as an unbroken string and breaks separately. A user-approved break is protected. If only layout reflow is authorized, words and punctuation remain unchanged. Do not abbreviate a venue or drop a condition to produce a neat block.

Leading is the vertical relation between lines. Tight display leading can create a powerful unit, but diacritics, ascenders and descenders must not collide unintentionally. Body-like information needs a rhythm compatible with the line length and density. A fixed numerical ratio is not a universal solution.

## Spacing and optical corrections

Tracking adjusts overall spacing; kerning adjusts a pair. Dense uppercase may need air; connected script should not be spread mechanically. Do not use excessive tracking as a universal premium signifier.

Optical alignment responds to perceived edges. Rounded or diagonal characters can require different positioning than flat stems. Punctuation can create visual imbalance even when bounding boxes align. A raster generator may approximate such instructions; inspect the output rather than claiming exact typography control.

Avoid fake precision: “perfect 12.5pt kerning” is not a usable image prompt. Specify observable intent, for example compact but separable letters, a stable baseline, no collisions, and consistent word spacing.

## Polish and multilingual text

Preserve ą ć ę ł ń ó ś ź ż and uppercase counterparts. Inspect actual glyphs, not just overall resemblance. Also preserve quotation marks, dash choice, case, decimal separators, currency position, phone grouping, date notation, URLs and proper names.

Treat translations as separate copy items with their own approval. A shorter translation can change meaning; do not introduce it while solving layout. Do not assume all scripts share Latin spacing or direction. RTL shaping, mixed-script numbers and line order require appropriate expertise and an actual supported route.

A generator's “multilingual” capability is not proof that every language or small glyph is reliable. Font coverage and shaping are independent requirements in an editable master.

## Expressive type versus functional type

A cultural title can tolerate deliberate distortion when the creator chose that reading friction. A medical instruction, event address or qualifying price condition usually cannot. Establish which words are allowed to be experiential and which must be immediately recoverable.

Do not route every price or date to DTP automatically. A large three-line offer may be a plausible raster. A forty-item menu with exact prices on a small story is different. Assess actual space, consequence and type burden.

## Accessibility and contrast

Check the actual background under each text item. Texture, local highlights and image edges may reduce reading even when a palette seems high-contrast. Do not rely solely on color to distinguish categories; labels, shape, ordering or spacing can carry the difference.

For applicable digital work, W3C contrast guidance is a reference for measured text contrast, not a guarantee produced by naming colors. Ratios and scope must be measured and applied correctly. WCAG is not a complete print-production specification. A claim of certified accessibility needs more evidence than this visual review.

Source for this bounded digital distinction, checked 2026-09-21: https://www.w3.org/WAI/WCAG22/Understanding/contrast-minimum.html

## Feasibility and repair

- Compact: credible space and hierarchy, followed by exact output inspection.
- At risk: plausible, but a named issue such as narrow columns or mixed scripts needs targeted review.
- DTP required: editable exact font, production master, mandatory precision exceeding the fixed raster constraints, functional codes or dielines.
- Unknown: a missing material viewing/production fact prevents a final claim; ask narrowly.

A failed letter in an otherwise correct poster may justify a source-bound edit if supported and authorized. Reinspect all text afterward. If repeated correction changes other strings or source properties, return to the accepted base and change method. Never call an approximation good enough for a required string.

## Production note

Hand off exact strings, approved breaks, hierarchy, type behavior or supplied font files, viewing context, formatting constraints and unresolved licensing. Do not claim the font is embedded, licensed or editable without checking the actual deliverable.
