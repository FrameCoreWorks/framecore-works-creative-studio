# Formats and campaign adaptation

Owner: commercial-visual-campaign-director. Version: 0.1.0-dev.1. Updated: 2026-09-21. Stable synthesis from static-design deliverable profiles, Poster Creator META and campaign direction sources. Platform specifications are volatile and must be checked separately.

## A format changes the reading problem

Separate the canvas ratio from the viewing condition. A tall poster at a venue, a tall phone story and a roll-up share verticality but not viewing distance, obstacles, UI overlays or detail burden. Do not simply move the same design into three rectangles.

Resolve the requested deliverable, dimensions/ratio if known, channel, approximate use size or distance, required copy, actual logo/source assets and success criterion. Missing safe-area specifications are Unknown, not an excuse to invent official platform values.

## Format decisions

| Deliverable | Design priorities | Typical adaptation | Critical check |
|---|---|---|---|
| Poster | Recognition and selected interpretation, then event/offer facts | Strong focal relation with a readable practical level | First read at intended distance, exact title/date |
| Flyer | Useful scan and closer reading | Group details, preserve item-to-time or item-to-price relations | No omitted terms or false alignment |
| Roll-up | Height, distance, audience movement and physical obstruction | Put essential recognition above likely obstruction; simplify lower detail | Actual stand/trim specification, not guessed universal safe zone |
| Horizontal banner | Rapid message and limited vertical depth | Use lateral relation between sign, copy and action | Title and CTA survive the actual placement |
| Feed social | Thumb-scale recognition plus optional closer scan | Prioritize one entry, then compact support | Actual preview/crop and mobile reading |
| Story/vertical digital | Tall composition with interface interference | Reposition information away from verified overlay regions | Do not call arbitrary margins official safe areas |
| Event screen | Large field seen in a room, sometimes from angles | Clear scale, few competing details, consistent series system | Real screen ratio, brightness/contrast conditions |
| POS/shelf | Product/offer recognition among competing objects | Direct product truth and clear offer hierarchy | Correct variant, qualifying terms, no invented discount |
| Business/membership card | Recognition first, then quick retrieval; use deliberate alignment and negative space rather than treating the card as a miniature poster | Exact supplied name, role, organization, contact details, member number and mark; invent none of them | Confirm whether the requested output is a concept raster or production master. Check contact strings at intended physical size; trim, bleed, editable type, exact logo vectors and print files require real specifications and appropriate DTP/prepress |
| Civic/social/political communication | State what the viewer should understand or do and identify who is speaking; make the required information directly comprehensible | Use only the approved position and supported facts. Never invent a statistic, organization, endorsement or documentary evidence | Clarify an unconfirmed speaker, stance or charged symbol before using it in a final direction. This is a design/source-truth check, not a legal-compliance assessment |
| Outdoor | Short viewing opportunities and large-scale reproduction | One strong message with essential support only | Real placement and production constraints |
| Cover | Promise and recognition at thumbnail and use size | Title/image relation and protected names | Front/spine/back scope, no unrequested mockup |
| Program/menu | Structured retrieval and factual relationships | Stable columns/groups and repeat rules | Density may require deterministic typesetting |
| Packaging concept | Brand/product/variant and panel geometry | Distinguish front-label concept from whole-pack artwork | Dieline, regulatory text, barcode and seams remain production work |

A format name does not decide production intent. A card can be a concept raster; a poster can require an editable master. Treat these as independent axes.

## Build a campaign system

After a master direction is accepted, identify:

- fixed: the message mechanism, exact copy, brand/product identity, distinctive relation;
- variable: crop, scale, line placement within allowed breaks, information grouping, secondary field;
- conditional: translations, alternative offers, shortened copy, changed hero. These require explicit selection where they alter content or meaning.

Write one transferable rule. For example: an actual repaired joint becomes the hinge between title and practical information. The joint and hinge relation are the system; a particular centered layout is not.

For each requested format, state how the rule changes spatially. In a tall poster the hinge can divide upper/lower fields; in a wide banner it can separate left/right. Copy remains exact, but its organization may change within allowed breaks. Do not add new slogan variants merely because the canvas is wider.

## Do not mistake consistency for cloning

Consistency can come from proportion, a particular type/image relation, repeated material logic, shape behavior or a stable tone. It does not require identical coordinates.

Review each variant individually and as a family. A crop can preserve the palette while destroying the defining relation. A new title size can preserve reading while changing the intended voice. Name which invariant passed or drifted, rather than saying “on brand” without evidence.

Before declaring that a locked layout cannot fit a new ratio, inspect the actual source geometry and clarify what the lock preserves: source coordinates, relative positions, crop, scale or the communicative relation. Check whether a permitted crop, uniform scaling or padding can retain all mandatory content and legibility. Without an image, dimensions, content bounds or sufficiently precise constraints, feasibility is unknown; do not infer impossibility from the change from portrait to square alone. If measured bounds and stated restrictions really conflict, name that conflict and ask only for the smallest necessary trade-off.

## Dense information and constrained formats

If required information does not fit:

1. Reconsider grouping and relative priority without deleting content.
2. Check whether a different crop or composition creates enough reading space.
3. Offer a specific content or format change for approval if necessary.
4. Route to typesetting/DTP if exact precision is the real requirement.

Do not shrink mandatory terms into unreadable texture, substitute pseudo-text, remove dates or invent a QR code as a way to hide the problem.

## Production distinction

For a prompt, semantic ratio and protected territories are design intent. For a delivered raster, inspect dimensions and the actual visual. For print, the operator must resolve effective resolution, trim/bleed, profile, font handling and finishing with the actual supplier.

Effective PPI is pixels divided by the physical length in inches for each axis. Metadata saying “300 DPI” does not create detail. Upscaling is an operation, not proof of new source information.

## Adaptation handoff

Return a small per-format map: requested ratio/dimensions, first read, layout change, preserved invariants, copy/break permissions, known safe regions and remaining test. Include only requested variants. Do not imply that a map is a set of generated or exported files.
