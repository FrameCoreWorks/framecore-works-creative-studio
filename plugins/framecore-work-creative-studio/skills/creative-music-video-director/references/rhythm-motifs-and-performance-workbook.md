# Rhythm, motifs and performance workbook

Use this for a selected music-video direction that needs a stronger rhythm relationship, a meaningful recurring image, sustained performance, or a repair to a generic treatment. It is a direction workbook, not an analysed song, shot list or generation interface. The original exercises below are `local_synthesis`; no actual music or render was inspected for them.

## 1. Keep three clocks distinct

| Clock | What it describes | Evidence required before making exact claims |
|---|---|---|
| Musical | Audible attacks, phrases, sections, silence or changes of density | Actual inspection or a supplied map, with source status |
| Performative | Preparation, gesture, attention, hesitation, breath or recovery | Proposed behaviour for a treatment; actual observation for a render claim |
| Editorial | Cut, hold, reveal, repetition or a transition between images | Proposed direction now; actual timebase and sequence downstream |

They may agree, counterpoint or remain independent. Decide which relationship the audience should notice. A visible gesture may carry an accent while the camera holds. A camera move may continue across a musical change. Neither requires a cut. Exact event placement stays `Unknown` until supported by the song and timing basis; a provisional treatment can still state its intention clearly.

### Rhythm relationship palette

| Choice | Direction instruction | Failure to watch for | Bounded correction |
|---|---|---|---|
| Shared accent | Let one meaningful action land with a selected audible event | Every sound gets a decorative image change | Reserve alignment for the action that matters |
| Preparation before accent | Begin the visible intention before the musical event, complete the action near it | Event appears unmotivated or requires impossible speed | Give preparation its own readable interval |
| Delayed response | Let the body or image respond after the sound for a stated effect | Delay reads as an accidental sync failure | Make the waiting behaviour perceptible; verify timing later |
| Sustained hold | Keep composition and attention through changing music | Constancy becomes absence of intention | Strengthen a visible attention task without adding arbitrary cuts |
| Counterpoint | Keep picture calm while music grows, or the reverse | Contrast exists only as an adjective in the treatment | Specify the actual unchanged and changing properties |
| Selective recurrence | Repeat an action/image when its meaning can be re-read | Repetition feels like filler coverage | Change one relation, or make deliberate sameness the thesis |

This palette is a set of options, not a progression. No quota of cuts, motifs, sections or changes is required.

## 2. Build a motif as a rule

A motif can be an action, spatial relation, material event or refusal, not just an object. Write its first readable function, what remains recognizable, what may change, and why its return matters. Avoid declaring that a prop universally means grief, freedom or rebirth; the film must establish its use.

```yaml
motif_contract:
  visible_event: "what a viewer can actually perceive"
  first_function: "what it does in this particular direction"
  recognisable_constant: "approved object, relation, gesture or framing"
  allowed_change: "one purposeful dimension, or deliberate constancy"
  musical_relation: "supplied/inspected basis, or conceptual only"
  absence_rule: "when omission matters, otherwise N/A"
  acceptance: "what must be legible without explanatory backstory"
```

Do not promote a concept prop into an identity carrier. An approved motif description tells downstream owners what to preserve; independent requests still need the appropriate real references when strict visual continuity is required.

## 3. Worked direction A: The place left open

**Hypothetical supplied material:** the user describes the song as a steady pulse that becomes more texturally crowded and later thins again. Their personal association is repeatedly straightening a place at a table for someone who has not arrived. They want an artist-present performance film with no literal narrative explanation and no visible singing. No audio, lyric text, artist biography, measured timing or section map has been inspected. Musical claims below are `user_report_only`; all image choices are proposals.

**Direction thesis:** the artist repeatedly makes one empty place ready, while the film tests whether that action is welcome, habit or self-protection. The empty space remains part of the composition; its meaning comes from the performer's behaviour.

**World:** one modest room and one table. An empty chair stays opposite the performer. A plain folded cloth and an unused glass define the empty place. Soft daylight has a consistent direction. No room transformation, phantom guest, floating objects or explanatory flashback. Styling is unresolved until approved; no invented artist persona is implied.

**Artist and camera relationship:** the performer gives attention to the empty place rather than performing distress for the lens. Repeated preparations remain careful and practical. The camera is an observer with a stable view of performer, hands and empty place. The direction does not demand new angles to mark musical changes.

**Image engine:** the hand starts to correct the cloth's alignment; later corrections become smaller even when the user-described music grows denser. During the described thinning, the hand approaches the same edge, stops, and remains beside it. The place stays open. This is an interpretive option, not a claim about the song's actual emotional intent.

| Motif decision | Full direction commitment |
|---|---|
| Visible event | Hand adjusts the cloth edge at the unused place |
| Recognisable constant | Same empty place and practical gesture, without prop multiplication |
| Allowed change | Smaller correction, then a visible decision to leave the edge as it is |
| Relation to music | Broad counterpoint to the user's described density; individual gestures need not hit every pulse |
| Viewer effect | The repeated task becomes a choice rather than automatic waiting |
| Boundary | No inference that the artist has experienced abandonment; no claim of exact timing |

**Emotional/rhythm shape:** begin with an ordinary practical action that establishes the task. Preserve enough continuation for repetition to be noticed. Let the increasingly small corrections remain restrained through the reported denser music. The final refusal to correct is not a triumphant release; it keeps the room, posture and composition materially similar. The exact musical locations remain open for the sequence owner after a real map is supplied or inspected.

**Distinctiveness test:** remove the repeated preparation of an empty place. If the treatment becomes merely “a sad artist in a room,” the central action has been lost. A viewer should be able to notice the artist's changed relationship to the task without a caption explaining whom they are waiting for.

**Risk and repair:** the stopped hand may be too subtle at the intended framing. First make the hand's prepared approach and deliberate stop readable. Keep the restrained performance and stable camera. Do not add tears, a disappearing chair or faster cutting as an automatic repair.

**Complete handoff note:** preserve the empty-place relationship, non-singing performance, cloth-adjustment rule, stable observational camera, consistent daylight and unresolved ending. The source for music shape is the user's description, not inspection. Sequence may propose event ordering and timing after confirming song evidence; it must preserve the fixed-camera option. Board panels may show distinct temporal states in the same framing. Video-prompt-architect owns motion feasibility and final prompt mapping. If exact identity is later required, obtain and bind its actual carrier; do not silently weaken the requirement. Audio Production Director receives music/audio questions only if requested; a treatment alone requires no audio production packet.

## 4. Worked direction B: a performance that stays with the listener

**Hypothetical supplied brief:** the artist asks for a deliberately sustained performance to accompany a song they describe as intimate, without a climax, montage or symbolic objects. They want direct address without pretending to sing in sync. No audio or performance reference has been inspected. This brief is `user_report_only`; no lip-sync verification is possible.

**Complete treatment:**

> Hold one stable portrait composition. The artist attends to the lens as though remaining present for one listener, rather than delivering a lyric to it. Allow natural blinking, breathing and small changes in focus; do not schedule a gesture for each musical phrase. Their mouth remains at rest except for ordinary natural movement, with no attempted lyric performance. The image maintains its distance, light and spatial relationship through the whole selected passage. The viewer's experience is continued company, not anticipation of a visual reveal. End while that relationship is still intact, at a point selected against the actual supplied passage during sequence planning.

**What makes it specific:** the user's deliberate request for sustained presence and no climax is the design authority. It does not need a symbolic object or novelty event. Identity, framing, duration and styling still need their actual approved sources if execution is requested.

**Acceptance:** the performer appears attentive without a repeated mechanical head motion or an imposed emotional escalation. Constancy is allowed. Review the real moving image over a relevant range; a set of attractive frames cannot prove sustained presence.

**Possible revision:** if the user reports “it looks like a freeze-frame,” retain the composition and remove any instruction that freezes the body. Direct one continuous attention task with natural micro-movement. If instead they report “it turns into fake singing,” preserve the non-singing intent and refer the actual clip to video for evidence-bounded diagnosis. Do not promise exact lyric sync as a repair to a task that did not request it.

## 5. Repair a treatment at the responsible level

| Symptom | Check first | Local correction / owner |
|---|---|---|
| Beautiful images could fit any song | Is a supplied association or musical quality doing real work? | Rewrite the image engine here; do not ask the prompt owner to decorate it |
| Every beat receives a cut | Was this asked for, or added as habit? | Choose which clock leads; keep meaningful holds |
| Motif returns but means nothing different | Is sameness intentional and perceptible? | Clarify its function, change one relation, or remove redundant returns |
| “More emotional” became “more hectic” | Which viewer relationship was actually requested? | Change attention/performance rather than automatically increasing motion |
| Fixed-camera treatment acquired varied angles | Did the handoff confuse board readability with coverage? | Restore accepted framing in sequence/board handoff |
| Concept depends on a drop nobody verified | Is the musical event user-supplied, inspected or invented? | Keep a conceptual relation; remove unsupported exact placement |
| Render misses the approved gesture | Is direction clear but execution weak? | Video owner reviews actual motion; do not replace the accepted thesis prematurely |
| New song edit changes the timing | Which dependent artifacts used the old version? | Mark sequence, cues, prompts and captions `review_required`; keep unaffected direction locks |

If the user rejects the central image, revise that device rather than producing palette variations. If the user accepts the direction but dislikes a rendered motion, preserve the direction while the video owner diagnoses the clip. Audio triage belongs to Audio Production Director and does not require a production packet; static-image criticism remains with output-critic-iteration.

## 6. Research anchor and transfer limits

Primary creator source: [Walker Art Center, Michel Gondry 2007 dialogue](https://www.walkerart.org/reader/michel-gondry-2007/), published 2020-04-22; [full transcript](https://s3.amazonaws.com/wac-imgix/cms/Michel-Gondry-Regis-Dialogue-Formatted.pdf), event 2007-06-23. Read 2026-09-28. Scope: a filmmaker's retrospective account, not a timing standard or generator manual.

**Source → rule → example:** Gondry describes changing his relationship to cutting on beats and using off-beat choices → choose a deliberate relation to rhythm instead of treating beat cutting as mandatory → A retains a stable view through described density changes; B sustains attention without scheduled accents. This transfers one general decision principle. The room, actions, motif, treatments and repair tests are original local synthesis and do not reproduce his videos, characters, imagery or shot order.

This historical source does not establish a current trend, audience outcome, artist biography or tool capability. Keep live public research proportionate to the actual new brief; a bundled workbook does not replace that gate.
