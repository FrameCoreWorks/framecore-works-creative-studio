# Persona, performance, motifs and reference authority

Use this reference when artist presence, choreography, image recurrence, styling or references materially shape the music-video direction. These are project decisions, not universal checkboxes.

## Persona as visible behavior

Translate only supplied or approved persona information into what the camera can observe. Depending on the film, describe gaze, posture, gesture, movement quality, relationship to space, proximity to camera, vocal/body connection or whether the artist is performer, narrator, protagonist, observer, symbol or absent. A lyrical adjective such as “mysterious” needs a visual behavior only if downstream work depends on it.

Do not invent biography, private motivation, identity facts, endorsements, cultural background or an artist's “signature” from a name or reference. The user may decide which characteristics are hard locks, allowed transformations or still open. If wardrobe or performance changes, define the cause and any continuity cue only when it matters to recognition or concept.

## Camera, body and audience relationship

The camera may observe, follow, pursue, approach, confront or share the performer's point of view. Choose one relationship at a given moment for a reason. It can shift across the film or remain constant. State the relevant behavior: distance, side, movement, steadiness, whether the performer leads or resists it, and what the viewer can see.

Movement can be still, micro-gestural, fluid, choreographed, forceful, ritual-like or intentionally awkward. Dance is not a default requirement. For performance, describe a playable action rather than only naming an emotion: hold eye contact while the room changes; keep the body still and let only the hands interrupt; look past the lens as if addressing someone outside frame. These are examples, not stock prescriptions.

Style and wardrobe can be functional, expressive, fashion-led, minimal or changing. Tie any change to a real conceptual or narrative cause. Do not use body exposure, transformation or “iconic presence” as an unexamined default. Preserve factual identity/product/wardrobe details from approved references where applicable.

## Motifs that carry meaning

A recurring element may be visual, spatial, bodily, action-based, color/material-based or editorial. It earns repetition when each return changes scale, context, intensity, perspective or completion, or when deliberate sameness is the point. Before keeping a symbol, ask:

- What does it do in this specific work?
- Can its effect be sensed in the image without an explanatory essay?
- Does its appearance, change or deliberate absence affect how the viewer reads the direction?

Use only enough motifs to make the visual language coherent. A one-off object is not automatically a symbol. Repetition is not automatically a motif. Do not require minimum recurrence, symbol counts, transformation, or an icon frame. “What would be remembered?” can diagnose distinctiveness, but a deliberately quiet work need not invent a poster image.

## Reference authority, scope and originality

Type each supplied reference by what it controls, for example:

| Role | Can inform |
|---|---|
| Persona / performance | User-approved identity facts, behavior or energy |
| Camera / motion | Framing, viewpoint, movement relationship or choreography |
| Visual style / material | Palette, contrast, texture, light or image treatment |
| Wardrobe / object | A specific garment or prop only within its stated role |
| Rhythm / edit | Hold, cut, sync, anti-sync or transition behavior |
| Symbol / world | A visual relationship or conceptual quality |

Record scope (whole work, section, moment), authority (directly controlling a specified property or inspiration-only) and conflict/override rules when they matter. A reference does not automatically license copying its entire frame, style signature, sequence, costume or story. Translate broad attributes into an original design for the user's project; never imitate the recognizable style of a living artist or copy a reference video shot-for-shot.

When references conflict, resolve the exact property and scope rather than blending everything. User-approved locks and source truth take precedence over an inspiration-only reference. If a strict identity or continuity constraint lacks a real carrier for a later independent generation, state that limitation for the sequence/prompt owner instead of suggesting that prose alone guarantees visual continuity.
