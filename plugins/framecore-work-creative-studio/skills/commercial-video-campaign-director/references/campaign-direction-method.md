# Campaign direction method

This reference explains how to turn a commercial brief into a distinctive time-based idea and a usable campaign direction. It is a decision method, not a fixed questionnaire, an output-length requirement, a formula for virality, or a substitute for current platform research.

## 1. Read the brief without inflating it

Start from the user's actual request. Separate known facts, approved claims, observed assets, interpretations, hypotheses and unknowns. The user may have a product, a rough thought, a finished script, an existing key visual, several references or a locked concept. Continue from that actual state; do not restart discovery or make them approve the same thing twice.

Use only the intake that can change the creative decision:

| Decision area | Useful evidence | If missing |
|---|---|---|
| Communication job | What should a viewer notice, understand, feel or do? | Ask one concise question or show a provisional interpretation. |
| Audience/context | A supplied audience, situation, behavior or observed barrier | Label any inferred context as a hypothesis; do not stereotype from demographics. |
| Product/brand truth | Observable feature, documented fact, real use, approved claim or brand asset | Keep the route at the level supported; ask for a fact only if it is essential. |
| Copy and offer | Exact user wording, approved line, CTA, offer and language | Preserve locked text; mark assistant copy as proposed and unapproved. |
| Deliverable/placement | Platform, placement, paid/organic context, objective, duration or asset family | Do not invent a placement rule. Verify it if the direction depends on it. |
| Production reality | Available footage/people/products, required assets, budget/time only when supplied | Flag the feasibility question; do not assume production capabilities. |

Do not confuse “the user says the product is premium” with substantiation for a comparative or quality claim. Do not treat a visual reference as proof of product performance, audience behavior or brand ownership. Ask for material source conflicts to be resolved; otherwise label the direction provisional.

## 2. Find the communication tension

The central idea often begins with a gap between what the viewer assumes, does, needs or notices and what the product, service or brand can truthfully show. The tension may be practical, sensory, cultural, emotional, contextual or purely visual. It need not be dramatic or framed as a pain point.

Keep the evidence status visible:

- **Supplied fact:** explicitly provided or approved by the user.
- **Observed detail:** directly visible in a supplied reference or described asset.
- **Research-supported:** tied to a relevant, current and attributable source.
- **Hypothesis:** a plausible creative interpretation to test, not a fact about an audience.
- **Unknown:** not established; avoid building the route around it.

Do not manufacture a buyer tension just to fill a template. If the brief has no substantiated audience insight, build from the product's observable use, a supplied occasion, a meaningful contrast, or the user's own associations. If none exists, ask one useful question.

## 3. Write a motion thesis before style adjectives

The thesis is a short statement of the idea's causal logic:

> Because [supported situation or tension], the film lets us see [specific event or change], so the viewer can understand/remember [truthful communication job].

This is an internal construction aid, not mandatory final copy. Test it:

1. **Specificity:** Could this premise sell any unrelated product with only a name change? If yes, find the real use, truth or tension.
2. **Motion necessity:** What changes through time? What does progression, action, reveal, rhythm, transformation or sound make understandable that a static key visual cannot?
3. **Truth:** Which part is established, observable, approved or merely hypothesized?
4. **Viewer value:** What does the viewer gain: recognition, useful demonstration, sensory access, emotional resolution, memory, a clear next step, or something else actually relevant?
5. **Ownability:** Which one concrete choice makes the idea recognizable as this campaign rather than a stock execution?

“Cinematic”, “scroll-stopping”, “premium”, “authentic”, “high energy” and “viral” are not a thesis or a creative device. They may describe a secondary quality only after the underlying idea works.

## 4. Choose an idea mechanism

A creative device is how the idea unfolds, not a visual style label. Select one primary mechanism that can be executed honestly. Examples of mechanism families include:

- reveal a useful detail that changes how an ordinary action is seen;
- stage a before/after state only when both states and the causal link are real;
- correct a supplied misconception without implying unsupported superiority;
- make an observable product action the proof, not a fabricated outcome;
- return to a distinctive gesture, object, sound or composition to create memory;
- contrast two experiences without naming or misrepresenting a competitor;
- make a real ritual, constraint or texture carry the narrative;
- use a human source only when their identity, expertise and statement are genuine and approved.

These are options, not a mandatory library. Do not select a device because it is trendy or fill every campaign with the same “unexpected hook”, macro reveal or talking founder. State what in this brief justifies it and what its main risk is.

### When proposing multiple directions

Deep work may compare up to three routes if there are several genuinely viable mechanisms. Give each route a distinct:

- source of tension or viewer question;
- primary event or action;
- proof/reveal logic;
- emotional or memory outcome;
- practical tradeoff.

Change the premise or mechanism, not merely the soundtrack, palette, camera move or casting. Do not brainstorm options when the user already selected the route or requested one.

## 5. Build attention as a progression, not a timer template

Describe what the viewer can perceive as the idea develops. Choose only the stages that the route needs, for example:

- **Opening event:** an immediate product-relevant action, state or question; not an arbitrary jump cut.
- **Orientation:** enough context to make the event legible; sometimes the opening already does this.
- **Development:** a decision, demonstration, contrast, escalation, change or sensory discovery.
- **Proof/reveal:** a visible, sourced or explicitly approved truth; no claim created by editing.
- **Payoff/memory:** a resolution, useful takeaway, ownable cue, emotional beat or honest CTA.

These are functions, not required beats. A five-second loop, a quiet product film and a longer story may need different structures. Do not impose fixed hook seconds, percentage rules, logo timing, cut counts, duration bands, age-to-tempo formulas or an obligatory hook/body/CTA architecture. Duration is a creative target only after the objective and the exact placement/workflow are known.

If a duration is user-specified, shape the information around it but do not claim it is optimal. If it is not specified and the structure materially depends on it, propose a clearly labeled working target or ask one question. Mark duration estimates as estimates; actual spoken-word timing or edit rhythm requires a script/performance or edit.

## 6. Direct an audiovisual system

Campaign-level audiovisual direction should explain which choices serve the thesis:

- **Movement:** what initiates, resists, changes or resolves; who/what moves and why.
- **Camera:** the relationship to the event: observe, reveal, accompany, compare, inspect, or hold; do not use movement as decoration.
- **Performance:** action, attention, gesture and emotional scale appropriate to the idea. Do not manufacture documentary authenticity or endorsements.
- **Image language:** framing, space, depth, texture, light and transitions that clarify the event and preserve product/brand truth.
- **Sound:** voice, ambience, music, designed sound, silence or a sound-off route. Sound can carry meaning, but do not depend on it unless the target context supports it. Do not assume copyrighted or licensed music is available.
- **Visible copy:** use only exact approved strings for locked copy. Treat unapproved lines as proposed; a campaign direction does not authorize rewriting legal copy or creating a claim.
- **Accessibility/comprehension:** consider captioning, contrast, sound-off comprehension and clear visual hierarchy where relevant. State a test, not a certification.

Avoid vague direction lists such as “dynamic cuts, epic music, premium lighting, cinematic camera”. Convert any retained quality word into an observable choice: what moves, what remains still, when attention shifts, which detail is revealed, what sound changes and what the viewer should perceive.

## 7. Give every campaign asset its own job

Only build an asset family when the user requests multiple deliverables or the brief clearly depends on them. Map each asset to its own viewing situation:

| Asset question | Decision |
|---|---|
| Who encounters it and in what context? | Cold scroll, consideration, product detail, launch announcement, reminder, event screen, or another supported use. |
| What single job does this asset perform? | Introduce, demonstrate, answer a specific objection, build memory, explain, direct, or retarget only if that is actually requested. |
| What must survive? | Product truth, brand cue, locked copy, required logo, offer or legal text, subject identity, narrative premise. |
| What should change? | Opening event, proof emphasis, performance, amount of context, CTA or crop-safe composition, when justified. |
| What needs separate research? | Current aspect/duration/destination rules, safe zones, ad objective, CTA behavior, audio/display conditions for that exact surface. |

Do not assume a key visual can be animated into every placement, that a 16:9 cutdown is simply cropped to 9:16, or that an aspect ratio alone determines layout. Adapt hierarchy, staging, action and text-safe space around the communication job and the actual destination. Preserve common campaign DNA without making all assets identical.

## 8. Research current constraints without confusing them with craft

Perform the Studio's active web-search preflight for substantive work. Search:

1. the exact platform, placement and ad-creation workflow named by the user;
2. the relevant campaign objective or asset surface when it changes behavior;
3. current official specification/help pages for hard requirements;
4. official creative recommendations separately from technical requirements;
5. original campaign examples or reputable practitioner evidence only when inspiration or practice is part of the question.

For each material claim, record its source, access/check date, exact scope, publication/update date if visible, and confidence. Classify it as **specification/requirement**, **platform recommendation**, **observed example**, **empirical finding**, or **creative inference**. A recommendation is not a universal law; a top-performing ad is not causal proof; an old study does not set today's platform specification; one placement's rules do not govern every product. Cite sources close to the relevant claim in the user-facing answer.

Research should inform a real decision, not become decorative browsing. Prefer first-party/current sources for platform constraints. Seek reputable and attributable practice evidence only when useful; do not imply “users commonly do X” from anecdotes or cherry-picked examples. If sources conflict or the exact workflow is unclear, say so and avoid merging values. Never promise performance, virality, conversion lift or compliance from a creative pattern.

## 9. Compact worked method example

The following is a synthetic reasoning example, not a recommended campaign or factual product claim. Assume a fictional café supplies one verified fact: its seasonal drink is finished with a visible piece of flame-toasted orange peel. No audience research, efficacy claim, price or approved headline is provided.

- **Job:** make the seasonal drink memorable and show what distinguishes its preparation.
- **Tension:** no buyer problem is established, so do not invent one.
- **Thesis:** the film turns the finishing gesture into the reason to pause and remember the drink.
- **Device:** a single sensory ritual: the peel chars, is expressed over the cup, and the fragrant finishing action resolves the film. Do not claim taste effects that were not supplied.
- **Motion:** one deliberate gesture and a visible change; close framing earns its place because the supplied fact is the finishing detail.
- **Sound:** real preparation sound is a possible design choice, not guaranteed source audio; create an intelligible sound-off visual version if the placement merits it.
- **Copy:** none is approved; the direction does not invent a menu title, claim, price or CTA.
- **Assets:** if requested, one version may introduce the ritual, another show the full preparation for product consideration, and a reminder may use the returning orange-peel/steam cue. Verify actual placements before setting lengths, layouts or safe zones.
- **Acceptance:** the viewer can identify the drink's supplied finishing action; the image does not imply an unsupplied ingredient or flavor claim; each cut has its own purpose; the route is not interchangeable with a generic beverage montage.

The method remains valid if the actual café, fact, format or idea changes: user evidence determines specificity.
