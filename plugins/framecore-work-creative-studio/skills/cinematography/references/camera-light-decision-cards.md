# Camera And Light Decision Cards

Use these cards to decide shot size, lens feel, movement and light from the brief, one beat at a time. Each card asks what the viewer must notice, lists the choices with what each one does to attention, and names an observable test for QA. The cards are a method, not a style list: derive every choice from the objective, the subject and the format, and write the reason next to it in Cinematography Notes. Vocabulary and physical tests stay in [video craft](../../video-prompt-architect/references/video-craft.md); generator syntax stays with the prompt owners.

## How to use a card

1. Name the beat's single job: show, prove, feel or transition.
2. Answer the card's question from the brief, the direction and the supplied references. If the answer is unknown, say so and pick the most readable option.
3. Record the choice, the reason and the test in one line, for example: `medium close, eye level: the hand and label must read together; test: label legible at phone size`.
4. Check the choice against its neighbours with the [adjacency workbook](../../storyboard-sequence-architect/references/shot-adjacency-workbook.md) before handoff.

## 1. Shot size: how much world does the beat need?

Question: what is the smallest frame in which the viewer still understands the action and its context?

| Choice | What it does | Use when | Risk |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Wide / establishing | Places subject in space and scale | Location, scale or relation matters to the message | Subject and text become too small on phone screens |
| Medium | Body language and an object in use | Action with hands, gesture or product handling | Neutral; can feel flat without a clear action |
| Close-up | Emotion, texture, one detail | One feature, face or reaction carries the beat | Loses context if it opens a sequence without a wider shot |
| Extreme close / macro | Material, mechanism, surface truth | Texture, finish or a precise mechanism is the claim | Abstract; can imply detail that the real product lacks |

Test: name the one thing the viewer must read; at the delivery size it is readable within the beat's duration. For vertical formats, check it inside the platform safe zone.

## 2. Lens feel: how should space behave?

Question: should the space around the subject feel open, natural or compressed? Describe the effect, not hardware the user did not supply.

| Feel | Effect | Use when | Risk |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Wide-angle feel | Depth exaggerated, near objects loom, closeness | Immersion, energy, small spaces, point of view | Distorts faces and product proportions near the edges |
| Normal feel | Perspective close to the eye | Honest product shape, documentary or UGC tone | Can read as plain if nothing else carries mood |
| Long-lens feel | Background compressed, subject isolated | Separation, observation, calm premium tone | Flattens depth; movement toward or away reads weakly |

Depth of field follows the same logic: shallow focus isolates one element and hides the rest; deep focus keeps relations readable. Test: the product or face keeps its true proportions, and the element the beat is about is the sharpest one.

## 3. Camera position and angle: whose view is this?

Question: from where would the intended viewer understand or relate to this moment?

- Eye level reads as neutral and honest; keep it for claims and testimonials.
- Low angle adds stature; high angle adds overview or vulnerability. Use either only when that meaning serves the brief.
- Point of view puts the viewer in the action; it needs a clear owner of the view and a stable horizon.
- Keep the axis of action consistent across a scene unless a cut deliberately resets it.

Test: a viewer can say whose view the shot represents, and screen direction matches the previous shot or the reset is visible.

## 4. Movement: what does the move reveal or hold?

Question: what changes in the frame because the camera moves? If nothing is revealed, followed or emphasised, keep the camera still.

| Move | Job | Use when | Risk |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Locked / static | Lets action and text read | Copy on screen, demonstrations, comparisons | Static only works when the subject or light carries change |
| Push in / pull out | Directs attention toward or away from one element | A reveal, realisation or end card | Too slow wastes a short beat; too fast reads as a zoom |
| Pan / tilt | Connects two points in one space | Cause and effect, scale, following a gaze | Smears detail and text mid-move |
| Tracking / follow | Keeps pace with a moving subject | Journeys, use in motion, energy | Needs a stable subject position to stay readable |
| Orbit | Shows form and volume | Hero product, sculptural objects | Reveals surfaces that may not exist in references |
| Handheld feel | Presence, immediacy, UGC credibility | Creator or documentary tone | Fatigue and blur; breaks premium or legible-copy beats |

One primary move per short beat (see Decision Rules in the skill). Test: the move starts and ends on readable frames, and the end frame is the state the next shot needs.

## 5. Light: what must the light make true?

Question: which quality of the subject must the light prove or protect (shape, material, skin, mood, time of day)?

- Direction: front light flattens and clarifies; side light reveals texture and form; back light separates and adds glow but can hide the front.
- Quality: soft light forgives and calms; hard light adds drama and crisp shadows and exposes every flaw.
- Contrast: low contrast feels open and friendly; high contrast feels dramatic and selective.
- Motivation: name the visible or implied source (window, lamp, screen, sun) so light stays consistent between shots.
- Materials: reflective surfaces need controlled highlights; transparent ones need back or edge light; matte ones need direction to show texture.

Test: the material reads as the real material, skin tones stay natural unless the brief says otherwise, and the light source stays in the same place across a continuous scene.

## 6. Colour and texture: what should the palette carry?

Question: which colours are locked by brand or product truth, and which are free to carry mood?

- Lock product and brand colours first; build grade and set colours around them, never over them.
- Use one dominant temperature per scene; change it only on a story turn and say why.
- Texture (grain, haze, softness, sharpness) is a tone signal; match it to the channel and to the supplied footage the shot must cut with.

Test: locked colours match the references in neutral light, and adjacent shots cut together without a visible grade jump unless one is intended.

## 7. Readability check before handoff

- Every beat has one job, one primary camera behaviour and one readable action.
- Text, product and faces are inside the safe zone and not crossed by a move at the moment they must be read.
- No choice claims hardware, lens metadata or production facts that the user did not provide.
- Each choice has a reason tied to the brief and a test the reviewer can apply to the actual media.
