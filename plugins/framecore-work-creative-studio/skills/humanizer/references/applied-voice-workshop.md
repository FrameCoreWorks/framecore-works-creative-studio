# Applied voice workshop

Use when a brief such as “more human”, “more premium” or “more like us” does not yet describe observable writing choices. This original Studio method extends the existing copy contract. It does not authorize invented brand attitudes, experiences or claims.

## Calibrate from evidence, not adjectives

Take a short supplied sample and record what it actually does: who addresses whom; sentence length and variation; directness; vocabulary; humor; certainty; explanation density. Keep a sample-derived observation separate from a user's explicit preference. One playful line is insufficient evidence that every customer message should joke.

If no sample exists, make a provisional recommendation from the stated use. Do not announce that you have discovered the brand's true voice. The [voice worksheet](../assets/voice-worksheet.md) is optional; a small request can use one sentence of internal calibration.

| Requested quality | Make an editable decision | Avoid the unsupported shortcut |
|---|---|---|
| Warm | Acknowledge the situation and give a useful next step | Fake familiarity, invented memories or promises |
| Confident | State the supported action plainly | “Best”, “guaranteed” or inflated certainty |
| Expert | Explain the relevant distinction or condition | Jargon as decoration or an invented qualification |
| Concise | Remove duplicate framing while retaining conditions | Deleting the caveat that changes the offer |
| Personal | Use an authorized speaker and concrete perspective | Pretending the author used the product |

These are candidate moves, not compulsory traits. A solemn notice can remain formal; technical terminology may be necessary. Match the requested artifact, not a universal casual style.

## Revise in two passes

First make a small claim inventory: each explicit fact, implied outcome, testimonial, comparison and next action. Mark its source and scope. The combined image/headline may imply an outcome no line explicitly states. Preserve qualifications in the meaningful reading, not only as fine print.

Then change language at the authorized level. For wording-only work, retain the proposition and factual intensity. For a genuinely new angle, present it as a proposal. Read the proposed sentence in its destination: a title, spoken line, longer paragraph and support notice need different pacing. An estimated reading duration is not an audio test.

## Keep a caption request unambiguous

A social-post caption is authored copy. A verbatim media transcript or synchronized accessibility caption represents supplied or inspected speech and meaningful sound; it belongs with [Audio Production Director's audio evidence workflow](../../audio-production-director/references/audio-evidence-and-av-handoff.md). If the request is clear, route directly. If “caption” leaves the deliverable materially ambiguous, ask one narrow question. Do not paraphrase a transcript to make it more marketable or promote a planned script into words actually spoken.

## Research basis and limits

[Mailchimp's own Voice and Tone guide](https://styleguide.mailchimp.com/voice-and-tone/), accessed 2026-09-28, distinguishes a relatively stable voice from situation-sensitive tone. This supports calibrating context separately; Mailchimp's humor and personality are specific to its brand and are not imported here. The editing table, worksheet and fictional copy are original synthesis. Accessibility terminology and evidence rules are applied through the linked Studio audio contract rather than inferred from a marketing-copy style guide.
