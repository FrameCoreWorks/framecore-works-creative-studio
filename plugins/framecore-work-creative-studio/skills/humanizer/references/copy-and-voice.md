# Copy, voice and exact-text authority

Owner: humanizer. Version: 0.1.0-dev.2. Updated: 2026-09-24. Stable craft derived from static-design integrated copy, AI-First direction, UtoPAI dialogue practice and a bounded synthesis of the canonical Humanizer commercial-copy craft source. One method supports standalone text and copy developed inside a visual project.

## 1. Meaning before polish

Determine who is speaking, to whom, for what purpose, through which medium and with what verified information. A small local workshop, a museum notice and a direct product offer need different distances and rhythms. “Make it human” is not permission to introduce slang, private experiences or an invented opinion.

Find the specific proposition. If the only known fact is that a service repairs bicycles, do not invent same-day service, free collection or “the best mechanics in town”. An honest line about the actual service may be stronger than an unsupported differentiator.

Identify the audience's next useful action. It may be recognizing an event, finding a date, comparing an offer, understanding an instruction or feeling an interpretive tension. Not every artifact needs a CTA.

### Commercial copy strategy

Use this checkpoint for a substantive advertisement, commercial headline, slogan or offer message, not for every short edit. Resolve only what can change the angle:

- the audience and channel;
- the user's actual communication goal and requested action, if any;
- supplied product or offer truth and the proof that is actually available;
- a material audience tension or objection, but only when the brief supports it;
- voice, hard constraints and explicitly forbidden or unverified claims.

Keep these as internal decision cues, not a mandatory form. Ask a concise question only when a missing fact changes the promise, price, eligibility, compliance or action. Otherwise make a conservative, reversible assumption and label it where useful. An unknown proof point stays unknown; it is not converted into a confident benefit. Do not infer buyer psychology, a testimonial, urgency, a result or a brand attitude from the category alone.

Before polishing a line, name its strategic mechanism in plain terms: for example, a supplied fact/proof, a supported objection answer, a recognizable situation, a visible demonstration, a truthful contrast, a visual anchor or a plain offer. Compare mechanisms only when the user requests options or when choosing between them would materially improve the result. When comparison is useful, make the candidates different in why they matter to the audience, not merely in diction, tone, colour or intensity. Do not expose the route labels unless they help the user decide.

Named sales or writing frameworks are optional diagnostic tools, never required templates. Use one only when it helps resolve a concrete message problem without inventing pain, outcomes, urgency or steps the offer cannot support.

Select against audience fit, product truth, evidence, channel, voice, likely reading conditions, the requested action and the image/asset it accompanies. If the route itself is unsupported, generic or mismatched, replace the angle before line editing. If only wording is weak, refine the line without restarting the concept. Return only the requested number of candidates; one requested line means one, and a settled or locked line is not an invitation to branch.

If a campaign actually needs a line system, distinguish its headline, optional proof/support and action rather than adding all three by convention. Review the combined claim made by image, headline, proof, testimonial and CTA: separate lines can reinforce one unsupported implication even when each sounds mild in isolation. Do not treat a small qualifier as automatic repair for an overbroad headline. An approved string remains exact; selection of wording does not verify its factual truth.

## 2. Choose the text operation

| Operation | What can change | What remains protected |
|---|---|---|
| New copy | Wording, emphasis and route can be proposed | Facts, required phrases and user constraints |
| Refinement | Rhythm, clarity and authorized message choices | Meaning, factual basis, selected speaker and essential conditions |
| Exact correction | Only the specified replacement and necessary grammar within scope | Other strings, tone and structure |
| Translation/transcreation | Language and, if authorized, local phrasing | Approved meaning, names, facts and required conditions |
| Critique only | No final wording changes unless requested | Existing artifact and scope |
| Locked copy | Layout treatment only within permission | All words, spelling, punctuation, language and locked breaks |
| Deliberate no-copy | No visible strings | Text-free intent; do not add a logo or caption by convention |

Missing wording in a text-led brief is not the same as deliberate no-copy. If the user asks for a poster but provides only a theme, offer draft wording or ask the material question. Do not quietly treat an assistant-invented headline as approved.

## 3. Distinct copy routes

Develop the argument with the visual. Useful routes include a concrete observation or supplied proof, a consequence grounded in the brief, an invitation or plain offer, a supported objection answer, a recognizable specific scene, a demonstration, a truthful contrast, a change of perspective, a visual anchor, or a brand stance explicitly supported by the brand direction. These are mechanisms to choose from, not a checklist to exhaust. Keep a micro-story inside supplied facts; do not let it imply a real customer's experience. Synonyms and louder enthusiasm do not create different concepts.

Synthetic example, repair café with supplied facts: visitors bring one small household object; volunteers assess it; no guarantee of repair. These are fictional teaching inputs, not a real event.

- Observation: “Nie wszystko, co stanęło, skończyło.” This is a proposed expressive line and may be too indirect without context.
- Action: “Przynieś rzecz, której szkoda wyrzucić.” Plainly invites the relevant behavior.
- Perspective: “Jeszcze jedna szansa dla tej lampki.” Specific and warmer, but it narrows the image toward a lamp and should not imply only lamps are welcome.

A useful support line could explain assessment without promising success. “Naprawimy wszystko” is not an acceptable shortcut because the brief does not support it. A route is judged with its image and support, not as an isolated clever phrase.

## 4. Natural voice is credible distance

Use concrete verbs and nouns when they clarify meaning. Remove inflated openings, nominalizations, duplicated benefits and press-release padding. Preserve technical terms when the audience needs them.

Warmth does not require forced intimacy. Confidence does not require superlatives. A quiet factual statement can be human; a deliberately formal institutional notice can also be human. Do not impose a casual persona on the client.

Cadence should suit the medium. A title can be compressed. A short voice-over needs phrasing that can be spoken without awkward clause stacking. A caption can carry detail the visual headline cannot. Avoid turning every sentence into a three-part rhetorical list.

Do not add typos, broken grammar or random hesitations to imitate a person. Never claim the result is human-authored or optimized to defeat an AI detector. The standard is purposeful, natural writing.

## 5. Dialogue and spoken copy

Use this method for a requested wording pass or short spoken copy. When the
requested artifact is a scene with actions, tactics, subtext or story change,
[screenplay-story-architect](../../screenplay-story-architect/SKILL.md) owns its
narrative construction; do not replace a scene with standalone slogans. Route
lyrics, song sections and music-production packets to
[audio-production-director](../../audio-production-director/SKILL.md). Retain exact
approved lines and supplied facts through either handoff. A wording-only change
to a line does not authorize rewriting its surrounding story or song.

A spoken line belongs to a situation and a relationship. What is the speaker doing with it: asking, avoiding, reassuring, challenging, explaining? Write playable behavior, not an abstract emotion label.

Synthetic micro-example:

PERSON A: “Jeszcze działa?”
PERSON B, turning the object over: “Czasami.”

That exchange can serve a scene if the uncertainty matters. It is not a universal style of clever understatement. For an instructional ad, an explicit clear sentence may be better.

Read the text mentally for rhythm, but do not claim an actual audio listening test occurred. Estimate duration with room for delivery and pauses; no universal words-per-minute number guarantees a performance. Where exact runtime matters, rehearsal or an actual recording is the acceptance test.

## 6. Editorial gate

Check the actual text:

1. Message: can the reader understand the intended proposition?
2. Specificity: what belongs to this offer, event or scene?
3. Contribution: does each line add information or meaning?
4. Truth: which factual claims are made and what supports them?
5. Voice: could this speaker plausibly say this to this audience?
6. Rhythm: are emphasis, sentence length and transitions purposeful?
7. Medium fit: can the text be read or spoken in the intended conditions?
8. Action: is the requested next step clear and proportionate?

A substitution test is useful, not absolute: if the entire line/image relation fits unrelated offers with only a name changed, investigate the missing specificity. Functional CTAs such as “Zapisz się” can remain plain. Do not contort them to meet a novelty quota.

Reject unsupported urgency, unverifiable superiority, fake testimonials and invented personal memories. Qualifiers matter. “Do 50%” and “50%” are different offers.

## 7. Selection and copy lock

Present candidates as candidates. If the user selects one, carry it exactly. If the user supplies a final version, that instruction establishes the copy decision without another ceremonial confirmation.

A broad instruction to improve a line authorizes drafting. If that line will be rendered in a graphic, show the actual revised wording for selection before finalizing. An exact replacement supplied by the user is already selected; general creative delegation alone does not turn an unseen assistant-written slogan into locked copy.

Track each visible item when needed:

- stable ID;
- exact text and language;
- attention role and whether mandatory;
- authority: user-final, supplied-source-final, selected assistant draft or unselected draft;
- factual support separately from wording approval;
- allowed text changes and line-break rules.

Unselected alternatives are not part of visible copy. Do not send them to the generator “for inspiration”; it may print them.

Once locked, a Humanizer pass cannot quietly rewrite a phrase. A suggested improvement reopens that item's selection, not every other decision.

## 8. Multilingual control

Write in the target language rather than translating English slogan structures mechanically. For Polish, protect inflection, diacritics, word order and the client-approved register. Keep names, product labels, currency formatting, handles and URLs exact.

Translation and layout are separate operations. Shortening a translation to fit can remove a condition. Show the changed meaning and ask for selection when it is material. A bilingual design needs independent reading and approval for each string; do not hide the secondary language in unreadable microtype.

## 9. Claims and fact checking

User approval of wording is not independent verification of the claim. If a material public claim is contradicted or unsupported, flag the exact issue. Preserve the source text for review; do not silently mutate it. Seek an appropriate primary source or an approved bounded alternative.

Do not treat a claim in a visual reference as the client's claim. A competitor's price, certification or slogan is not transferable source truth. Legal/compliance acceptance is a separate responsibility where required; this skill does not certify it.

## 10. Delivery

Deliver actual wording in the requested format and quantity. Keep commentary short unless critique is requested. For a graphic, hand off the exact selected strings, hierarchy, break permissions and unresolved factual status. For copy-only, stop at copy; do not turn it into a full visual campaign without being asked.
