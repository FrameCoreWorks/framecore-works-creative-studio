# Shot adjacency and physical readability

Use after a direction is selected or when the user directly requests shots. Apply [the sequence contract](sequence-contract-and-continuity.md) and [reference authority](reference-authority-and-carriers.md) first. The campaign owner authors the idea; this owner makes its temporal construction readable. Research basis: [creative upgrade sources](../../research-evidence/references/creative-upgrade-sources.md), especially Adobe's action/sequence and match-cut explanations. The detailed checks and examples below are original planning methods.

## Read a shot as a visible event

Name an entry state, an observable action and an exit state. Identify the support/contact of each important object, what starts the movement and what can stop it. A moving highlight is not evidence that a material or identity has changed. A new viewpoint cannot prove unseen product anatomy from a single front photo.

For a short unit start with one primary action and one primary camera move. Additional events need a reason and enough time to read; an exception can be intentional. Distinguish subject movement, camera movement and an editing transition. Keep optics or focal lengths Unknown until supplied, chosen as a labelled creative proposal or verified; do not attach cinematic numbers to a concept as fake production facts.

## Review each pair of adjacent shots

| Property | Inspect in the planned outgoing and incoming states | Repair when it fails |
|---|---|---|
| Main attention point | Is the new subject findable after the cut? | Reframe, simplify competing elements or provide a brief settling moment. |
| Action phase | Does the movement continue, repeat intentionally, or restart accidentally? | Move the cut within the action or declare a deliberate temporal jump. |
| Screen direction and axis | Are left/right relations and gaze coherent for this scene? | Keep a stable axis or introduce a readable change of viewpoint. |
| Scale and position | Is the size/placement change intentional and understandable? | Adjust framing or use a clearly motivated insert. |
| Object and hand state | Are cap, lid, grip, fill, wardrobe and contact consistent? | Restore the agreed state; do not hide a mismatch with an effect. |
| Light and colour | Does a cut alter perceived product truth or merely the chosen light? | Protect true colour references; separate grading intent from factual shade. |
| Audio event | Does continuing sound help meaning or contradict the new picture? | Move, remove or intentionally bridge the cue; retain source and timing evidence. |

These are questions for editorial judgment. A matched number or a repeated prompt phrase does not establish a successful cut. Intentional discontinuity is allowed when it serves the brief and is made readable.

## Six bridge choices

1. **Action continuation:** carry a gesture through a changed angle. Need comparable direction, phase and speed. Do not duplicate the gesture across clips.
2. **Graphic relationship:** connect a shape, arrangement or dominant tone. Need a meaningful relationship; do not turn the actual product into another object unless authorised.
3. **Occlusion:** a foreground surface covers the outgoing view and reveals the next. Plan the coverage, source and reveal; a hand crossing the frame does not automatically hide an edit.
4. **Sound bridge:** let one sound precede or continue across a picture change. Record whether it is source sound, proposed SFX, VO or music. Do not call an unmeasured alignment frame-accurate.
5. **Plain cut with contrast:** let a clear change in scale or viewpoint organise the information. A cut can be the most legible choice; no transition effect is compulsory.
6. **Dissolve or overlap:** use when the overlap supports time, mood or a relationship. Account for overlapping time in the edit and inspect ghosted text, faces and labels.

## Worked 25-second planning example

Synthetic brief: show a dark-red nail colour and the supplied bottle; no performance claims. This example is a planning estimate and has not been rendered. It illustrates arithmetic and event logic, not a reusable mandatory storyboard.

| Shot | Edit interval | Visible event | Exit and next-shot relationship |
|---|---|---|---|
| S01 | 0–4 s | A hand rotates slightly to inspect the finished manicure. | The hand begins to lower; S02 continues the lowering gesture. |
| S02 | 4–9 s | The hand settles beside the unchanged, supported bottle. | Hold a readable spatial relation before a closer view. |
| S03 | 9–14 s | A controlled highlight reveals the bottle's existing curvature. | Plain cut to the actual colour reference; no bottle deformation. |
| S04 | 14–19 s | One close view makes the nail colour and natural surface readable. | Cut back to the product with consistent source truth. |
| S05 | 19–25 s | The bottle and hand resolve into a calm final composition. | End on the requested product view; no unsolicited text or CTA. |

All five intervals are edit durations. Generator clip lengths may differ; handles and trimming remain a separate plan. If a dissolve is introduced, adjust the actual timeline and its overlap explicitly. Do not merely add clip durations and assert a final runtime. No frame count exists until the timebase is known. Audio may later motivate a different cut rhythm; do not invent a beat grid now.

## Repair example

Failure: S01 ends with the cap attached; S02 starts with a bare brush already touching a nail, then the bottle floats during a camera orbit.

Diagnosis: missing state transition and unsupported contact, before considering style. Keep the cap attached throughout if opening is unnecessary, or allocate an explicit opening action. Support the bottle and choose either the brush action or the orbit as the dominant event. Preserve the accepted concept; repair the dependency that actually failed.

## Handoff and optional arithmetic check

Use [the sequence planning example](../assets/shot-plan.example.json) and [read-only checker](../../../scripts/review-creative-plan.mjs) when a structured plan helps. The checker can find interval gaps, duplicate IDs, missing bridges and declared reference blockers. It cannot judge pixels, anatomy, taste, physical simulation or perceptual smoothness. A structurally valid plan keeps observed quality uninspected until actual media is reviewed.

Pass shot IDs, target/edit timing, incoming/outgoing states, required bridges, protected product/identity properties, actual reference bindings and unresolved fields to the board and video-prompt owners. For new media, inspect each required output and then the actual adjacent pair. An accepted standalone frame is not evidence that its transition works. Repeat only the affected check after a repair.
