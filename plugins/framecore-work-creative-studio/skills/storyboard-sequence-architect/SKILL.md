# Storyboard Sequence Architect

Own the time-based plan: what happens, in what order, for how long, from which
camera position, and what state carries into the next shot. This is a practical
creative artifact, not a claim that a future render, edit, or continuity result
has been tested.

Run the mandatory [public research preflight](../research-evidence/SKILL.md) before substantive sequence or shot-planning decisions. Search relevant cinematic grammar, production context, or current generator limits; distinguish sourced facts from local creative inference and do not copy a reference sequence shot for shot.

Start at the user's requested stage. A clear script and deliverable do not need
another intake form. For a vague request, ask only for the decision that changes
the sequence materially, or provide a compact provisional plan with its
assumptions named. Keep story-authoring with
[screenplay-story-architect](../screenplay-story-architect/SKILL.md); do not
silently rewrite accepted story facts to make a shot easier.

Read the [sequence and continuity contract](references/sequence-contract-and-continuity.md)
for beats, timing, camera, shot cards, and continuity. Read
[reference authority and carriers](references/reference-authority-and-carriers.md)
when uploaded images, boards, clips, or strict continuity are involved. For a
visual board, use the distinct
[board architect](../storyboard-board-architect/SKILL.md). A non-temporal reference
or coverage board goes directly there; do not manufacture events, timing or
shot cards before it can compare the requested sources or properties. For final video
prompt compilation, hand off to
[video-prompt-architect](../video-prompt-architect/SKILL.md); named-generator
syntax belongs to that generator-aware route.

For a dialogue-led scene, a difficult action handoff or a revision that changes timing, use [the applied sequence workshop](references/applied-sequence-workshop.md). Its optional state/speech ledger and worked fixed-camera example protect intelligibility without inventing measured timecodes or requiring cuts.

## Deliver the requested structure

Use only fields that help the next decision. A useful sequence package can
include:

- **Sequence:** objective, source/version, intended duration, scope, and
  unresolved assumptions.
- **Beat map:** order, dramatic or informational function, visible change,
  and approximate time range where useful.
- **Shot cards:** stable IDs; framing/scale and viewpoint; subject and
  observable action; camera behavior; start and end state; duration estimate;
  dialogue/sound only if supplied or requested; continuity dependencies.
- **Timing:** planning estimate, measured value, or unknown. Never blur these
  states. Ranges must fit the declared target without false frame precision.
- **Camera logic:** why the angle, distance, movement, side, and ending frame
  make the action readable. Avoid camera motion used as generic decoration.
- **Continuity state:** property-level locks and an explicit carrier-readiness
  assessment for each independent request.
- **Promptability notes:** what can pass to image/video prompt compilation,
  what needs a real attached carrier, and what remains a creative or production
  decision.

Do not force every project into three acts, a fixed number of shots, timecodes,
or a board-grid preset. Use fewer, clearer shots when each is legible; split
when one view cannot establish geography and prove a detail. For estimates,
label the basis and round sensibly. If exact duration is unknown, provide
relative beats or ask whether a useful estimate is wanted.

When the user asks for both a sequence and a board, finish or label the
sequence contract first, then pass the approved shot order, IDs, exact labels,
selected frames, and unresolved items to the board architect. The board is a
separate layout artifact: it does not replace the sequence, nor does its
contact sheet imply frame-by-frame generation.

For edits, identify the changed fact, affected shots, protected elements, and
one acceptance test. Propagate only dependent changes. If the actual render is
not supplied, do not diagnose it as inspected. If a user requests a final video
prompt, hand off a complete approved shot card or the smallest useful shot set,
not the entire internal analysis.

## Before handoff

Check order and causal readability; shot purpose; duration estimate versus
target; spatial axis, eyelines and screen direction where material; prop,
wardrobe, identity and product state; exact dialogue/copy; reference roles;
actual carrier availability; and the requested output type. One primary
continuity issue should produce one targeted repair and a visible pass/fail
test. Read [handoff and delivery rules](../storyboard-board-architect/references/board-delivery-and-handoffs.md)
for the shared downstream contract.
