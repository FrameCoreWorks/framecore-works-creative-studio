# Applied board workshop

Use when deciding what a panel must prove and how the board will be read. This is original Studio synthesis. It does not establish new story events, certify image output, or replace the sequence owner's timing decisions.

## Choose one comparison question

| Purpose | Useful panel difference | Preserve |
|---|---|---|
| Temporal story | Before, decisive action, consequence | Accepted event order, shot ID and camera locks |
| Coverage comparison | Two views that reveal different needed information in the same state | Subject/prop state; do not invent elapsed time |
| Reference | Identity, wardrobe, material, lighting or approved alternatives | Property-level authority; no unrequested story progression |

Ask what a reader should learn by moving between panels. For a fixed-camera scene, changes in hands, posture or object state can be enough. For a material comparison, repeated framing can isolate the variable. Do not enlarge the panel count to fill a grid or insert fictitious events to make a reference board look cinematic.

Use the [panel map](../assets/panel-map.md) to pair each selected moment or property with its source and exact label. It is optional. Keep internal IDs separate from visible artwork copy. A label's presence in a working worksheet does not authorize printing it on the board.

## Make each panel carry evidence

Identify the decisive region before choosing its crop: a hand's contact, two people's relationship, a product seam, or a light direction. A beautiful crop fails if it hides the thing the panel exists to compare. If one panel must prove both tiny detail and wide geography, separate the views only when the requested purpose and source authority allow it.

Read the board at its intended use size. If labels become unreadable, change hierarchy, page grouping or delivery form. Do not shrink mandatory words into decorative texture. For a prompt-only board, give one complete layout/content specification and mark future image inputs as planned. Do not claim that a generated board or usable independent carrier exists.

## Distinguish board labels from accessible captions

[W3C's captions guidance](https://www.w3.org/WAI/media/av/captions/), accessed 2026-09-28, treats captions as synchronized representations of relevant speech and sound, distinguishes translated subtitles in its terminology, and requires checking automatic output for accuracy. A storyboard label or punchy social headline is not that artifact.

Applied here: preserve the supplied caption text; record which pictured action or face it must not obscure; and pass timing/source questions to Audio Production Director and actual video placement/synchronization to Video. Do not create final caption timestamps from still panels. Textual caption needs can be planned here without promising player behavior or accessibility conformance.
