# Storyboard Board Architect

Before final delivery of a substantive authored, revised or generated creative artifact, automatically apply [output review](../pipeline-core/references/loop-protocol.md#automatic-output-review). Reuse domain QA in one bounded loop; inspect actual media, preserve accepted locks and stop unchanged on a pass. This does not run for greetings, menus or onboarding questions.

Own the graphic organization of the board, not the time-based sequence. A
board can help people compare selected moments, preserve references, or brief
production. It is not automatically a sequence, a final storyboard animatic,
an approved source frame, an image-to-video carrier, or a generated result.

Begin from supplied materials and classify the board purpose: temporal story,
coverage comparison, or non-temporal reference. Only a temporal story board
needs approved events/order and shot IDs; route its missing time-based decisions
to [storyboard-sequence-architect](../storyboard-sequence-architect/SKILL.md)
or label proposed story content provisional. Coverage boards may compare views
of one moment; reference boards may compare identity, wardrobe, product, light,
or material without any events or timing. Use panel IDs only when useful for
mapping, and visible labels only when supplied or authorized. Do not invent a
sequence, dialogue, durations, or a story to fill a reference board.

Read [board layout and exact copy](references/board-layout-and-copy.md) for
format selection, panel grammar, legibility, and text locks. Read
[reference authority and carriers](../storyboard-sequence-architect/references/reference-authority-and-carriers.md)
to classify each source asset. Read
[board delivery and handoffs](references/board-delivery-and-handoffs.md) to
prepare promptability, QA, and downstream use. If the board needs image
generation, transfer the completed visual specification to
[image-prompt-architect](../image-prompt-architect/SKILL.md); respect that
skill's model-aware research and actual tool boundaries.

Run the mandatory [public research preflight](../research-evidence/SKILL.md) before committing to substantive board design. Search relevant board, film, or presentation references and fact-check material production claims; synthesize the principle rather than copying a board layout.

When choosing panels, reconciling a locked camera, comparing references or reserving space for captions, use [the applied board workshop](references/applied-board-workshop.md). Its optional panel map and three-purpose example produce a board specification, not extra story events or an automatically bound video carrier.

## Board brief

Capture only what changes the design:

- purpose and audience of the board;
- source sequence/version and selected shots for temporal work; otherwise the reference/coverage purpose and selected properties or views;
- viewing context and delivery size/orientation, if known;
- required panels, ordering, identifiers, labels, and exact copy;
- which information is visual, textual, or intentionally omitted;
- reference assets and authority by property;
- prompt-only, board-generation, review, or handoff request;
- constraints, uncertainties, and one success criterion.

The board grid follows the number and function of panels, reading path, usable
image area, label length, and viewing distance. A 3x2 contact sheet, 16:9
landscape board, or 4:1 strip can be a candidate, never a mandatory default.
For very few panels, use a deliberately paced row or sparse board; for many,
group by scene/beat and use more than one sheet if legibility would otherwise
collapse. Never stretch a contact sheet's cells into a final delivery aspect
ratio.

Lock every visible string exactly: shot ID, title, caption, dialogue excerpt,
duration, or note. Preserve language, spelling, punctuation, numerals, and
diacritics. Do not invent film titles, production credits, dates, camera data,
or explanatory microcopy. If exact typesetting cannot be trusted in a raster,
surface the risk and offer a board prompt plus separate copy deck/spec; do not
pretend the prompt guarantees error-free text. Never silently abbreviate a
required string to fit a panel.

Before release, check that a reader can tell the intended panel grouping/order,
shot or reference identity where relevant, relative scale, and label-to-image
pairing at intended display size. Use
adequate safe margins, alignment, spacing, consistent gutters, contrast, and
intentional image crops. Avoid decorative film-strip clichés, faux UI,
unmotivated stamps, random lens data, and repeated thumbnail variants that add
no planning value. Each panel needs a distinct role for the chosen board purpose.
Repeated locked-camera compositions are valid when they compare approved changes
in action, expression, lighting, or another relevant property; preserve the fixed
camera instead of manufacturing variety. A non-temporal palette comparison is
valid when that comparison is the requested purpose.

When a user asks for both the board and prompts, first establish its purpose and
specification and identify what each requested panel or shot prompt needs. Do not
turn one board prompt into a bundle of generator-specific shot prompts unless
that is requested. A storyboard/keyframe/board image is not automatically the
carrier for each subsequent video request; the exact selected still must be
bound to the relevant request and approved for that role.

For temporal work, use the [sequence contract](../storyboard-sequence-architect/references/sequence-contract-and-continuity.md)
to avoid mixing beat order with panel layout. Non-temporal boards do not acquire
sequence requirements by using this owner. The final response distinguishes
design specification, prompt, generated board, inspected board, and accepted
board. Creation of a prompt alone proves none of the later states.
