# Short-form timing card

One set of timing numbers for every video, voice-over and copy owner, so a script, a storyboard, a motion contract and a caption file plan the same seconds. Platform facts are dated and sourced; everything else is a Studio planning value: a working target to shape a draft, never a rule that overrides the brief, the placement's own specification or what the edit shows. Actual timing comes from the recorded voice, the performance and the cut.

## When the opening must work

| Placement | Platform fact (read 2026-10-11) | Working target for the opening |
| --- | --- | --- |
| YouTube skippable in-stream | The viewer can skip after 5 s; no maximum length, under 3 min recommended | The brand or product and the reason to keep watching inside the first 5 s |
| YouTube non-skippable in-stream | 60 s or shorter (15 to 60 s by campaign subtype) | One idea; the brand early, because it cannot be skipped and should not feel like it |
| YouTube bumper | 6 s at most (at least 5 s), not skippable | One idea; hook and payoff can be the same image |
| YouTube Shorts ad | Under 60 s recommended; the viewer can swipe away at once | A readable first frame and the hook by about 1.5 s |
| Vertical feeds: TikTok, Reels, Stories, Shorts, Meta feed | No fixed hook rule from the platforms | A readable first frame (it is the thumbnail), the first readable moment by about 1.5 s, the hook complete by 2 to 3 s, legible without sound |
| Pre-roll on a website, TV, cinema, a screen in a shop | The placement's own specification | Follow the delivery spec; no feed rule applies |

Source for the YouTube rows: Google Ads Help, "About video ad formats" ([answer 2375464](https://support.google.com/google-ads/answer/2375464?hl=en)), read 2026-10-11; recheck it before a paid campaign. Feed sizes, safe areas and copy limits are in the [channel snapshot](../../research-evidence/references/channel-specs-snapshot.md). The motion critique warns when the first readable moment comes after 1.5 s.

These targets apply to paid and organic short-form placements. A brand film, a loop, a quiet product film or a story may open slower on purpose: say so in the brief, and the [campaign direction method](../../commercial-video-campaign-director/references/campaign-direction-method.md) decides the structure.

## How many words a voice-over can say

Spoken time is the length minus silence: about 0.5 s before the first word and the end-card hold after the last. Budget = spoken seconds × rate, rounded down.

| Rate | English | Polish |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Calm (premium, explainer, children) | 2.2 words/s | 1.8 words/s (about 4.5 syllables/s) |
| Standard (most ads and UGC) | 2.6 words/s | 2.1 words/s (about 5 syllables/s) |
| Fast (offers, energetic UGC) | 3.0 words/s | 2.4 words/s (about 5.6 syllables/s) |

Polish words are longer than English ones, so the same message needs more time in Polish; plan the Polish script to the Polish row, never to a word count translated from English.

| Length | End-card hold | Spoken time | English words (calm / standard / fast) | Polish words (calm / standard / fast) |
| --- | --- | --- | --- | --- |
| 6 s | none to 1 s (0.5 s used) | 5 s | 11 / 13 / 15 | 9 / 10 / 12 |
| 15 s | 2 s | 12.5 s | 27 / 32 / 37 | 22 / 26 / 30 |
| 20 s | 2 s | 17.5 s | 38 / 45 / 52 | 31 / 36 / 42 |
| 30 s | 2.5 s | 27 s | 59 / 70 / 81 | 48 / 56 / 64 |
| 60 s | 3 s | 56.5 s | 124 / 146 / 169 | 101 / 118 / 135 |

These rates are Studio planning values from common broadcast and dubbing practice, not a measured standard for every voice. Measure the real read: time the voice actor or the text-to-speech take, and let the word timings decide. [Caption Studio](../../caption-studio/SKILL.md) reports reading speed for captions, and the voice tools (`motion-sync`, [animate-voice](../../hyperframes-workflow/assets/animate-voice/README.md)) give words per second for a recorded voice.

## On-screen text and captions

- **Text in the picture:** the longer of 13 characters per second plus 0.5 s and 0.5 s plus a third of a second per word, at least 1 s, from the moment the last word has landed ([motion craft](../../hyperframes-workflow/references/motion-craft.md)). The contract checker, the craft critique and Caption Studio's burned-in profile use exactly this rule.
- **Subtitles:** characters per second by language and audience from the Netflix Timed Text Style Guide (Polish adult 17, English adult 20), at most two lines of 42 characters ([readability defaults](../../caption-studio/references/readability-defaults.md)).
- **One idea per scene:** at most about 10 words on screen at once in a vertical frame and 14 in a wide one (the critique's text-amount warning).

## End card and call to action

- Hold the final brand or call to action at least 1.5 s with nothing moving (the critique's warning level); 2 to 3 s when it carries an address, a code or a price.
- A spoken call to action ends before the end-card hold starts, so the last frame is read, not heard over.
- A loop ends on a frame that leads back into the first instead of an end card.

## Beats per length

The [beat budget](../../storytelling/references/short-form-structures.md#beat-budget) gives how many changes a length holds (6 s: 1 or 2; 15 s: 3; 30 s: 4 or 5; 60 s: 5 to 7). The UGC [script skeleton](../../ugc/references/formats-scripts-and-disclosure.md#3-script-skeleton) splits 15, 30 and 60 s into hook, context, proof, objection and call to action with the same opening targets as this card.
