# Finding directions

The shared method for ideas in graphics, video and motion. Use it for an open brief, a request for ideas or directions, "make it fresh", or after a rejected batch. Skip it for a directed task, an approved concept or an exact edit. A user with a precise concept gets it executed, with any additions only as labelled optional suggestions ([partner rule](../../workflow-orchestrator/references/studio-contract.md#partner-not-pusher)). Run it once per project: the chosen direction travels in Project State to every owner (poster, film, motion, copy), who executes it without starting the ideas again unless asked.

The goal is not novelty for its own sake. It is a direction that belongs to this client and could not be pasted onto a competitor, chosen on purpose rather than by habit. The method teaches how to build an idea from the brief and holds no ready ideas. Templates, styles and library records come in afterwards, to execute the idea that was chosen; none of them is a direction.

## Ready templates

Owner decision 2026-10-27 (replaces the 2026-10-11 rule against ready-made ideas): when the owner's library has templates that fit the brief, show them first, so the user sees at once how the work can look and work goes faster. A Studio original (blueprints, reel starters, runnable snippets, kits) or a starter whose source allows copying is used as it is or adapted to this brief, with its source named; a creator's own film or prompt (the Opus gallery and similar) is shown with its link and creator and adapted, never copied. Fill the remaining directions with the steps below; the brief and the user still decide which fits.

## 1. Name the obvious first

Work out this category's defaults yourself; they change with the category, the place and the year, so no list is kept here. Ask:

- What would an image search for the category return? That is the stock motif.
- Which colours do most competitors share? That is the stock palette.
- Which typeface would a sector template pick? That is the type habit.
- Which effect would a template apply to it (a line drawing the logo, spin and shine, particles, slow motion, a fade between product shots)? That is the stock move.
- Which line would every competitor's ad say? That is the stock claim.

When the client's name is itself one of these (the word for the category's material, tool or symbol), picturing it literally is the most obvious idea of all: list it with the defaults, and let the name lens work on the letters, the sound or a second meaning instead.

Write the four to six answers down. They are a tool, not a ban: a default comes back only as a decision backed by a fact of this client, never by habit. Tell the user in one line, in their language, what you set aside and why (every competitor uses it).

## 2. Collect real material

What belongs to this client only: a fact about the product or how it is made, a place, a time of day, a customer ritual, a contradiction, a number, a constraint, the name itself (its sound, its letters, its meaning). Unknown facts stay unknown and are asked for or labelled; never invent one to make an idea work. Aim for about eight to twelve such facts before generating; each one is also a picture to find or a reference to search for, so the same list later feeds the imagery.

## 3. Generate through lenses

A lens is a question that turns material into an event. Push the material through several lenses; each direction comes from a different one.

| Lens | Question | The move |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Process and time | What happens before, during or after that nobody sees? | List the steps and the hours; take the one with tension or surprise and make it the event |
| Consequence | What does the result change for a person? | Follow the customer an hour or a day later; show the change, not the product |
| Absence | What is missing without it? | Take the product or service out and show the gap it leaves |
| Borrowed format | Which format from another world could carry it? | Find a format with rules of its own (a timetable, a forecast, a manual, a scoreboard, a receipt) whose rules match a fact of the brief; its rules become the structure |
| Scale | What changes very close or very far away? | Go to the detail nobody looks at, or out to the street, the city or the year |
| The name | What can the name, its sound or its letters do? | Read the name aloud and letter by letter, and look for a second meaning; give one letter, its sound or that meaning an action (a literal picture of a name that is the category's motif is a default) |
| Type as action | Can the words alone perform the idea? | Give the key word a verb from the brief and let the type do it |
| Cultural observation | What do these customers really say or do? | Note a phrase, a gesture or a habit from the real situation; ask when you do not know it |
| Constraint | Which single rule would give a strong style? | Choose one limit (one line, one colour, one material, one camera move) and make every frame obey it |
| Material swap | Which other material could tell it? | Tell it in a material tied to a fact of the brief instead of the product's own surface |

## 4. Build the mechanism

An idea becomes a direction when it has a mechanism: something changes, and the change means something for this client. Write it in one sentence: "X becomes Y, because Z" for video and motion, "X meets Y, which shows Z" for a still. If you cannot write the sentence, you have a mood or a look, not an idea yet.

## 5. Spread three directions

- Three by default; respect a number the user gives. Directions differ in mechanism (what happens) and in source (which lens), not in palette, font or effect.
- They differ in argument too: for each, write in one sentence what the viewer concludes. If two of these sentences could swap places, rebuild one around another truth of the brief; a new metaphor, name or reveal order on the same argument is not a new direction.
- At most one direction uses the category's defaults, and it is labelled as the deliberate classic. At least one breaks them: nothing from the step 1 list in its motif, palette or move.
- Each in one sentence: what we see, what happens, why it fits this client. The look (palette, type, style) follows from the idea, never from the category.
- For several assets (a poster, a reel and a logo animation), each direction covers all of them, so the user chooses one idea for the whole campaign.

## 6. Test before showing

When several rounds converge on the same argument despite different styling, optionally explore a wider spread of mechanisms within the existing candidate/QA budget. Ask for distinct source material, premises or transformations, then compare brief fit. Verbalized Sampling is an optional research-informed prompting technique, not a default quality gate: any model-stated probabilities are exploratory estimates, never a measured chance another model would produce the idea or proof of originality. Do not expose invented percentage rankings. Prefer fewer defensible directions to a forced quota. See [creative judgment and sources](quality-improvement-methods.md#creative-judgment-with-external-evidence).

- **Swap test.** Put a competitor's name on it. If the direction still works unchanged, it is generic: rework or drop it.
- **Brand test.** Tell apart what fits the category, what uses a true trait of this product and what only this brand could own. When the brief gives no fact that sets the brand apart, ask for one, or say plainly that this part stays open; never invent a history, a patent or an advantage to pass the swap test.
- **Removal test.** For every effect, and above all one added when the user asks for "stronger": what does the viewer stop understanding if it is removed? If nothing, find a better event or relation instead of adding particles, moves or transitions.
- **Retell test.** Could someone describe it in one sentence after seeing it once?
- **Stopping test (ads and reels).** What does the viewer see full-screen in the first second, and what is the one big moment? An idea that stays small (a document, a card, a list in one frame) is clever but quiet; give it a striking event, scale or picture, or choose a direction that has one.
- **Fit test.** Does it serve the goal and the audience, keep the offer and the name readable, and can it be made with the real assets and tools (code motion, a generator, the user's photos)?
- Clarity beats strangeness: a fresh direction that hides the name or the offer fails.

## 7. Recommend and carry

Recommend one direction with a concrete reason and one trade-off. In direct production of a complete brief, run steps 1 to 6 internally, make the strongest direction that is not a default unless the brief asks for a classic, and say in one line what was set aside. Record the chosen direction, its lens, its mechanism sentence and the set-aside defaults in Project State, so later stages and revisions keep them.

## Build it in the medium

For product films, link physical actions whose cause, direction, contact, timing and product behavior read across shots; let material, shape, use and sound motivate transitions, not generic macro shots or a fixed hook/body/CTA formula.

The direction is made with the means of its medium, not described and then decorated.

- **Graphics.** One frame carries a relation: two things whose meeting says something (a contrast of scale, a crop that hides and reveals, type and image doing one job, an empty area that points). Decide what the eye reads first, second and third; the name and the offer have their place in that order from the start, not at the end.
- **Video.** An event in time: a cause, a turn and a payoff, seen from a chosen point of view. Decide what the camera knows and when the viewer learns it. Sound can carry the cause or the payoff. A shot stays only if it moves the change forward.
- **Motion graphics.** A transformation: what becomes what, under one rule invented for this brief (it folds, it settles, it is counted, it is stamped). Draw the first state and the last state so that each says the idea, then design the path between them: timing that makes the change felt, one signature move only this idea could have, and an ending that lands the name or the offer. A style, a template or a library record only dresses this path; it never replaces it.

## Worked derivation

Shown once to make the steps visible; its ideas are not for reuse, and its facts are assumed for the illustration (in a real brief they come from the user). A watchmaker's shop wants six seconds of motion for its opening.

1. Obvious, set aside: clock faces and gears, gold on black, a serif with flourishes, hands spinning fast, "time for quality".
2. Material: repairs take two weeks; most watches brought in belonged to someone's parents; the shop sits in a passage by the station.
3. Lenses: consequence (an inherited watch runs again on another wrist), borrowed format (the repair ticket, whose stamped dates become the timeline), constraint (only the single second in which the watch starts again).
4. Mechanism: "A stopped second becomes a moving one, because someone repaired it." The film is that one second, stretched to six.
5. Tests: a competitor could also claim a restarted watch, so the ticket direction, with the shop's real two weeks and its station address, passes the swap test better; both retell in a sentence; both can be built in code motion without photos.
