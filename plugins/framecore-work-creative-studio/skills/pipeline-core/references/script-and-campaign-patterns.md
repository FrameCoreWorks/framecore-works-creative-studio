# Script and campaign patterns

Shared patterns for scripts, short-form video, campaigns, launches and sales conversations, used by every owner that writes for a business: Copy Voice, Story And Screenplay, Storytelling, UGC, Marketing, Sales Campaign Strategy, Video Campaign, Shot Sequence, Video Prompt and the Motion Graphics.

These are structures with reasons, not finished scripts. The idea still comes from the brief and the [ideation method](ideation-method.md); a pattern says what each part must do, and the owner writes it for this client, in the user's language and market. Patterns compiled from common short-form and direct-response practice, including a creator's prompt collection shared in 2026 (KANJI.LOW); expressed here in Studio's own words, with no text copied.

## Honesty rules for every pattern

- No invented proof: statistics, testimonials, case studies, results, scarcity and deadlines come from the user or a checked source, through Research when they are public claims.
- Agitation names a real problem in the audience's words; it never exaggerates harm or fear.
- Scarcity, deadlines and guarantees exist only when they are real.
- Sales conversations persuade by understanding; no pressure tactics, no false urgency, no manipulation of vulnerable people.

## 1. A script that production can use

Write every short script as beats, one row each: **time**, **spoken line**, **on-screen text**, **visual change**, **proof shown**, **CTA** when present. This format goes straight into Shot Sequence's shot cards, Video Prompt's generator prompts and the Motion Graphics's boards; the words decide what is heard, the visual column decides what is generated or animated.

- A visual change every 2 to 3 seconds in talking or live-action video; 1 to 2.5 seconds per board in motion graphics ([reel board system](../../hyperframes-workflow/references/reel-board-system.md)).
- Spoken sentences of up to about 10 words; one idea per sentence.
- Every claim also appears on screen, so the piece works muted.

## 2. Hooks

The first line decides whether the rest is seen. Write it under about 8 words, readable on screen within the first second, and make the body keep its promise.

| Trigger | What it does | Fails when |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Identity call-out | Names who it is for ("Jeśli prowadzisz salon…") | The group is too broad to recognise itself |
| Curiosity gap | Opens a question the body answers | The answer never comes or is trivial |
| Contrarian claim | Challenges a common belief | It cannot be backed or is contrarian for show |
| Number first | Leads with a specific number | The number is invented or vague |
| Mid-action confession | Starts inside a moment ("Właśnie zepsułem…") | The moment has no link to the point |
| Incomplete visual instruction | Shows a step and withholds the result | The result does not pay off |
| Pain + timeframe + twist | A pain, a clock, and a belief that flips | The twist is an adjective, not a belief |
| Outcome first | Shows the end result, then how | The result is not real or not shown |

Write two or three hooks on different triggers for one body when the user tests ads; rank them by how fast they are understood, not by how loud they are.

## 3. Retention

- Cut intros, greetings, thanks, disclaimers that can move to the caption, and slogans in the middle.
- Open a loop early and close it before the end; never leave it open as bait.
- Put the strongest proof where viewers usually leave (just after the hook).
- End on a line worth saving or repeating.
- A soft CTA in the middle works only after value has been given.

## 4. Proof the viewer can see

The best proof is visible in the frame, not asserted: a live demonstration, a measurement, a before and after under the same conditions, a real customer with consent, a document or a screen. In UGC, use problem, live demo, visible proof, reaction. If proof is missing, say what the user must supply; never fake it.

## 5. Story structures

| Structure | Beats | Good for |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Moment of change | before, the moment, after, what it means for the viewer | founder and brand stories |
| Failure to method | what went wrong, what was learned, the method now | expertise and trust |
| Customer transformation | situation, obstacle, turning point with the product, result, the customer's words | case studies (consent and real results only) |
| Tension ladder | each beat raises the stakes until the turn | longer stories and ads |
| Tiny story opener | three sentences: situation, complication, question | hooks for any topic |
| Story to lesson | a short story, then the transferable rule | education and thought leadership |

## 6. Teaching formats

- **One concept in 60 seconds**: the concept, why it matters, one example, one action.
- **Mistake-led**: the common mistake, its cost, the fix shown.
- **Myth versus fact**: the myth, the checked fact with its source, what to do instead.
- **Analogy first**: a familiar comparison, then the real mechanism.
- **Beginner to advanced ladder**: the same skill at three levels.
- **Whiteboard**: the idea drawn step by step (a natural motion graphics piece).

## 7. Calls to action by audience temperature

| Viewer | Ask | Examples |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Cold (does not know the brand) | Low commitment | save, follow, comment a word, watch the next part |
| Warm (knows the brand) | A step closer | send a keyword in a message, get a free resource, sign up |
| Hot (ready to buy) | The transaction | book, call, buy, visit |

One CTA per piece. Bridge to it: name the next problem the page, the call or the product solves; "link in bio" is never the whole reason. In a paid ad, the platform's CTA button carries the action and the creative names the benefit.

## 8. Series and repurposing

- **Series**: one promise across 5 to 7 episodes; each episode stands alone, ends with a reason to watch the next, and has the same visual frame.
- **Long to short**: find self-contained moments in long material (a claim with its proof, a story with its turn), give each its own hook and on-screen text, and check that nothing loses its context.

## 9. Launch sequence

| Phase | Content |
| --- | --- |
| Teaser (several days) | the problem, behind the scenes, the people, a waitlist or interest list |
| Announcement | what it is, who it is for, the one main benefit, how to get it |
| Proof | demonstrations, early users with consent, numbers that are real |
| Objections | answers to the questions people actually ask (a FAQ in short videos) |
| Last call | only when a real deadline or limited quantity exists |

Map each phase to formats (reel, story, post, email, ad) in Marketing's plan; the visual system stays one across the launch.

## 10. Sales conversations and follow-up

- **Discovery call**: permission and agenda; the client's situation; the problem in their words; its impact; what success looks like; whether and how Studio's user can help; the next step agreed together.
- **Diagnostic questions**: open questions in a branching order (situation, problem, impact, priority, decision process), each answer choosing the next question.
- **Objections**: acknowledge, ask what is behind it, answer with a fact or an option, check whether it is resolved; keep a short library of the client's real objections and honest answers.
- **Follow-up after no reply**: short, adds something useful each time (an answer, an example, a resource), at most two or three, then a polite closing message that leaves the door open.

## 11. Video sales letter for cold traffic

Hook; the problem in the audience's words; why the usual fixes fail; the mechanism that makes this offer work, explained plainly and truthfully; proof; the offer; risk reversal only if a real guarantee exists; the CTA. Keep agitation factual and short, and test the claims with Research before the script is ready.

## 12. From script to generated video and motion

1. Each script beat becomes a shot card (Shot Sequence): subject, action, camera, duration, continuity carriers, the proof it shows.
2. Exact text (names, prices, claims, CTA) is an overlay or a motion board, never generated inside an image or clip.
3. Generator prompts come per shot from Video Prompt; motion boards from the Motion Graphics; voice and music from Audio Production.
4. The visual change cadence of section 1 holds across generated shots and boards.
