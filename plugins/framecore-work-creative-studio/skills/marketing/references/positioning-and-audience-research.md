# Positioning and audience research method

Read before writing positioning, audiences or a campaign thesis that is not already supplied and approved. It is a method, not a set of ready answers: every finding comes from the user's material, a source Studio can name, or is marked as an assumption. Current market facts (competitor prices, platform rules, statistics) go through the [Research](../../research-evidence/SKILL.md) gate.

## 1. Questions to answer, in this order

Ask only what the material does not answer, one material question per reply (Brief Architect's rule); answer the rest from sources and mark the gaps.

1. **What is it, in one plain sentence?** What the offer does, for whom, in the user's words before any marketing language.
2. **Who decides and who uses?** Buyer, user and influencer can differ (a parent buys a children's class; a manager approves software that a team uses).
3. **In which situations does someone look for it?** Moments with a trigger ("before the first day at a new job", "after a failed delivery"), not demographics alone.
4. **What do they use instead today?** Direct competitors, substitutes and doing nothing. Positioning is always against an alternative.
5. **What is different, and can it be shown?** Each difference needs a proof the audience can check: a demonstration, a number with its source, a guarantee, a credential, a customer's real words.
6. **What stops them?** Objections, risks, price, effort, switching cost, trust.
7. **Why now?** A real reason (season, regulation, a new feature, a deadline). If there is none, say so; do not invent urgency.
8. **What may never be said?** Claims the user cannot prove, legal limits, the brand's own taboos.

## 2. Evidence ledger

Keep one table for the project and carry it to every downstream owner. A positioning line or a message may only use rows whose status allows it.

| ID | Statement | Source | Date | Status | May be used for |
| --- | --- | --- | --- | --- | --- |
| E1 | {what is claimed} | {document, page, interview, analytics export, with link or file} | {date read or recorded} | verified / user-stated / inferred / unknown | {headline, body proof, internal only, nothing} |

- **Verified:** Studio read it in a source that can be cited (the user's data, a public page, a document).
- **User-stated:** the user said it; usable with attribution in internal strategy, and in public copy only when the user confirms they can prove it.
- **Inferred:** Studio's reasoning from other rows; label it in the strategy, never present it as fact in copy.
- **Unknown:** needed but missing; list it with who could answer it.

Source strength, strongest first: the user's own data and documents (sales, support tickets, reviews they received, analytics) > customers' own words (interviews, reviews, comments, with permission) > competitors' and substitutes' public pages, read and dated > published research with a named method > secondary summaries. Opinions in forums are signals of language and objections, not of size.

## 3. Audience as situations

Describe each audience as a row, not a persona story:

| Audience | Situation and trigger | Job they want done | Current alternative | Main objection | Proof that answers it | Evidence IDs |
| --- | --- | --- | --- | --- | --- | --- |

Two or three rows are usually enough; more rows than the campaign can serve dilute it. Mark which row is primary.

## 4. Positioning statement and its test

Write it plainly, then test it:

> For {primary audience in their situation}, who {need or trigger}, {brand or offer} is the {frame of reference} that {main difference}, because {proof}. Unlike {main alternative}, it {what the alternative does not do}.

Pass only if every clause has an evidence ID, the difference matters to the primary audience (they named it, or its absence is their objection), a competitor could not sign the same sentence, and the proof can be shown in the medium the campaign uses. A statement that fails one test is revised, not decorated.

## 5. Message hierarchy from the ledger

- **One main message** from the positioning statement.
- **Two or three supporting messages,** each tied to an objection row and a proof.
- **Proof points** with their evidence IDs and the wording limits ("up to", the date of a number, the sample).
- **Words to use and avoid,** taken from customers' own language where the ledger has it; the [brand voice guide](../assets/brand-voice-guide.template.md) records the result.

## 6. Output

The Brand Strategy or Marketing Plan carries: the plain-sentence description, audience rows with the primary marked, the positioning statement with its test result, the message hierarchy, the evidence ledger (or its ID) and the Unknown list with owners. Downstream owners (Graphic Design, Copy Voice, Video Campaign, UGC) receive the ledger, not a summary of it.

## What this method does not do

It does not supply ready audiences, personas, positioning lines or category insights for any industry, and it does not fill a gap with plausible-sounding facts. It never uses one user's market, rates or clients as defaults for another.
