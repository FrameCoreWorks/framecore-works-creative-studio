# Short-form story structures

Stable craft for reels, shorts, ads and explainers. The structures are starting shapes, not templates to fill: choose one from the asset's job, then test its causality. Authored scenes and dialogue go to [Screenplay Story Architect](../../screenplay-story-architect/SKILL.md); timed shot cards go to [Storyboard Sequence Architect](../../storyboard-sequence-architect/SKILL.md).

## Choose by the asset's job

| Structure | Beats | Fits | Avoid when |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Question and payoff | Open a specific question, show progress, complicate or prove, pay off | Most short-form; product reveals; explainers | The payoff cannot answer the opening |
| Problem, agitate, solve | Name a real friction, show its cost, resolve it | Direct response with a true, common problem | The pain must be exaggerated; health, money or fear claims without evidence |
| Before, after, bridge | Show the state now, the changed state, what bridged them | A real, showable change | The change is not real or not permitted to show |
| Demonstration | Outcome first, then how it happens | Tools, apps, recipes, techniques | The outcome needs long setup to be legible |
| Story spine | Once upon a time, every day, but one day, because of that (repeat), until finally, ever since then | Micro-narratives with a character; brand films | Under 15 s; it needs room for causes |
| List | Promise a number, deliver items in rising value, close | Tips, features, myths | Items are interchangeable filler |
| Reveal | Withhold the subject while each beat adds a clue | Launches, transformations | There is no reason to keep watching before the reveal |
| Myth and fact | State a belief, show evidence against it, give the better view | Education, category reframes | The evidence is missing or the myth is a straw man |
| Testimony arc | Situation, doubt, experience, observed result | A real, consenting speaker only | The speaker is synthetic or the result is not theirs |
| Loop | The end returns to the opening line or frame | Organic content built for re-watching | The ending must carry a CTA or a legal line |
| Sustained form | One mood, image or action held and varied | Mood pieces, music-led and observational work | The brief needs an argument or an offer |

The story spine is Kenn Adams's improvisation exercise; it is useful for finding causes, not for forcing a moral. Sustained and non-linear forms are deliberate choices; do not add conflict or a twist to fill a template (see the authorship boundary in [Storytelling](../SKILL.md#authorship-boundary)).

## Causality: "but" and "therefore"

Join consecutive beats with "but" or "therefore", not "and then", a rule popularised by Trey Parker and Matt Stone. Read the beat list aloud with those words. A beat joined only by "and then" either moves information without changing anything, or belongs in another video. For an ad, the product or brand must be one of the causes, not a card appended after the story ends.

## Beat budget

A planning heuristic; actual timing comes from the storyboard and the edit.

| Length | Beats | Notes |
| --- | --- | --- |
| 6 s | 1 or 2 | One idea; the hook and the payoff may be the same image |
| 15 s | 3 | Hook, one proof or turn, payoff with the brand |
| 30 s | 4 or 5 | Room for one complication or objection |
| 60 s | 5 to 7 | Room for a second turn; keep one question |
| 90 s and longer | Chapters of 20 to 40 s | Each chapter needs its own small question and payoff |

## Checks before handoff

- The first beat is legible without sound and states or implies the question.
- Every beat changes information, state or feeling in something seen or heard.
- One subject and one question carry the piece; secondary threads are cut.
- The payoff answers the opening question; a loop or sustained form is a stated choice.
- In an ad, the product's role is causal and the claim it makes is supported.
- The structure fits the length in the beat budget, or the deviation has a reason.
