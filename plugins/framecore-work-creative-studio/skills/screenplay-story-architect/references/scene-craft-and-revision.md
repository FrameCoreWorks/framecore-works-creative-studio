# Scene craft and revision

Use for writing or diagnosing an actual scene, dialogue passage, scene run or
revision. Read only the sections that address the current request.

## A scene's working unit

For a plotted scene, track just enough to understand its effect:

| Element | Question | Failure signal |
|---|---|---|
| Entry state | What can the audience see/hear when the scene starts? What is already true? | It begins with explanation that can be shown later or omitted. |
| Immediate task | What does a person or group do here, or avoid doing? | People are present only to repeat the premise. |
| Pressure | What practical, social, physical, relational or informational limit shapes the action? | A generic “conflict” is named but nothing resists anyone. |
| Tactics | What does each person try? How can that tactic be visible/audible? | A character has a feeling label but no playable behavior. |
| Response | What changes because another person, object, rule or event responds? | The scene is a series of unconnected beats. |
| Turn or intentional hold | Does new information, power, position, knowledge or attention shift? If not, what justifies remaining? | A turn is pasted in, or stillness is treated as an error by default. |
| Exit state | What is different—or deliberately remains unresolved—at the end? | The last line restates the theme, or the scene ends only because the outline says so. |
| Dependency | What fact, prop, relationship, time, sound or exact wording carries forward? | Later scenes silently reset it. |

For an observational scene, substitute attention and duration for a forced
goal/obstacle/turn: where the audience looks, what repeats, what gradually
changes, and what the frame allows us to infer. For essay/documentary work,
test evidence, point of view and the boundary between witnessed material and
interpretation. Use the right test for the selected form.

## Actions that play

Write what an actor can do and a camera/microphone can register. “She feels
betrayed” may be a useful author note; screenplay action should provide the
behavior that lets the audience form its own reading. Specific actions are
not automatically better when they have no relation to the scene.

- Prefer a meaningful verb and a clear subject over a chain of abstract
  adjectives.
- Give an object a physical state and location only when the state matters to
  the action or continuity.
- Let an action carry more than one reading when the material supports it; do
  not annotate every gesture with the intended emotion.
- Use pauses and holds where a performer or audience can inhabit them. Do not
  quantify an untested performance to an exact second as if it were measured.
- Avoid camera direction in a screenplay unless point of view, an unusual
  visual event or production need makes it material. Use a technical script
  only when the user asks for that bridge.

## Character, relationship and subtext

Subtext is not a hidden backstory attached to every exchange. It can emerge
from the mismatch between what someone asks and does, from a task that gives
the conversation somewhere to go, or from what both people already know.

For a dialogue scene, check:

1. What does each speaker want from this specific exchange, if anything?
2. What tactic or relationship history changes how they ask?
3. Is an answer direct, evasive, delayed, misheard, refused or physical?
4. Does the next line respond to the person, or only deliver writer information?
5. Can an image, prop, silence or action carry the information instead?

Dialogue that states facts is allowed when the character has a real reason to
state them and the audience needs the information. The failure is not
exposition itself; it is unmotivated explanation, duplicate information or
several characters speaking in the author's same polished voice.

Do not make every character witty, laconic, ironic or inarticulate. Match
sentence length, vocabulary, interruption, politeness, humor and silence to
this person's immediate situation and relationship. A character pass can be
useful: read one character's lines together, then test whether the voice stays
recognizable without making every phrase a catchphrase. Human voice is not a
collection of filler words.

## Dialogue and image check

After drafting, compare each spoken line against visible action:

- If the line merely names what is already obvious, try cutting it.
- If a line explains the theme, ask whether the action or consequence can do it.
- If a line must carry a fact, let the speaker's reason and timing shape it.
- If no line is required, do not add dialogue to make a scene feel complete.
- Read out loud when useful; a clean sentence on the page may still be
  awkward in a mouth. This is a proposal until performed by the intended voice.

For approved ad copy, VO, legal text, names or literal user dialogue, maintain
exact strings and order. Mark alternate rewrites as options; never silently
change locked wording while improving cadence.

## Diagnose before rewriting

| Layer | Evidence to name | First repair to consider |
|---|---|---|
| Premise/form | The concept has no specific attention or selects a mismatched mode. | Reframe the core question or form; do not polish every line. |
| Scene logic | The action does not cause, reveal, resist or deliberately hold anything. | Clarify the task, event relation or reason for duration. |
| Character | Choices are interchangeable or only serve exposition. | Change one tactic, habit, contradiction or relationship move grounded in supplied facts. |
| Dialogue | Lines duplicate image, sound alike or state what nobody would say here. | Cut, redirect, shorten, let a physical action answer, or distinguish voice. |
| Rhythm/edit | The scene arrives too early, runs past its effect or interrupts an intended hold. | Enter later, leave earlier, compare one edit alternative; preserve purposeful silence. |
| Continuity | Prop, geography, knowledge, wardrobe, time or consequence resets. | Identify the first wrong state, repair downstream dependencies, retain unrelated choices. |
| Production | Timing, locations, rights, cast, exact copy or carrier is missing. | Label the assumption or present a feasible alternative; do not call the draft tested. |
| Truth/claims | The script asserts an unapproved feature, testimonial or result. | Remove or mark it as a proposal pending verification; never let dramatic need create fact. |

Choose a correction with a visible acceptance test. Examples: “the character's
refusal is readable without the explanatory line”; “the newly moved prop is
still in the right hand in every later beat”; “the observed 12-second hold
still shows the repair detail continuously.” Do not turn a broad preference
into ten simultaneous changes.

## Revision propagation

Before changing a draft, record in brief:

- accepted intent, facts and exact text;
- observed problem and the layer where it originates;
- affected beat/scene IDs or passages;
- one proposed change and its acceptance test.

Trace changed state through later scenes. A changed decision can alter who
knows a fact, where an object is, the emotional reading of a callback, the
edit, or whether a line is still truthful. Update connected material only.
When the new version fails the test, keep the failure evidence and revise the
decision that caused it; do not accumulate contradictory patch notes.

If notes conflict, summarize the different audience effects they describe,
identify which align with the user's intent and offer a concrete choice. A
reviewer's note is evidence to consider, not an instruction to obey or to
discard automatically. Preserve a deliberate tradeoff after the user chooses.

## Original micro-example

Synthetic example for instruction only; no real person or product is implied.

An overnight building attendant has one minute to close a community print
room. The new volunteer keeps a misprinted sheet instead of putting it in the
recycling. The attendant's first line can be a rule (“Scrap bin's there.”) or
a practical question (“You need the back?”). The second version gives the
volunteer room to answer with an actual purpose; neither line should become a
universal model of “subtext.” If the user wants a quiet observational version,
the exchange itself may be unnecessary: the choice to carry the sheet out can
be the scene.

## Closing QA

Check the actual passage, not an imagined improvement:

- Is the intended form still intact?
- Can performers enact the important actions?
- Is the audience given the information it needs, with space to infer what it
  does not need explained?
- Does each exact locked string remain exact?
- Have the accepted elements survived the repair?
- Is every claimed continuity, read, performance or render check based on
  material actually inspected?
