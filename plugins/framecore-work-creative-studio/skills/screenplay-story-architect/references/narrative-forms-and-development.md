# Narrative forms and development

Use this reference to move from a seed to the smallest complete story artifact.
It is a decision method, not a universal formula or an intake questionnaire.

## Start with the material that exists

The seed may be an action, place, image, sound, line, relationship, product
truth, question, lyric, draft or production constraint. Identify its specific
interest before attaching a familiar genre pattern. A broad feeling such as
“hopeful” is not yet a scene: look for the behavior, object, setting or
contradiction through which the feeling could become observable.

Ask what the user wants the viewer to notice, feel, infer, remember or do. Do
not require autobiographical detail. When the user offers no personal material,
invent a small number of clearly labeled story proposals only if they help the
user decide. When a draft already contains an authored detail, preserve it
before proposing a more convenient replacement.

Useful checks:

1. What is particular here, rather than interchangeable with another brief?
2. What can the audience directly see or hear?
3. What meaning should remain open rather than explained?
4. Which fact or creative decision is still actually unresolved?
5. What is the requested artifact now?

If those answers are already clear enough, write. Do not hold a finished request
at the concept stage to complete a hidden worksheet.

## Select the story form before choosing its machinery

| Form | Organizing attention | Useful tests | Avoid imposing |
|---|---|---|---|
| Causal fiction | A person acts under resistance; consequences reshape what they can do next. | Are the important actions chosen and legible? Does the result of one beat affect another? | A twist, villain, trauma, “arc” or three-act map when the material does not need one. |
| Observational scene/film | Time, repeated behavior, space or a changing relation rewards sustained attention. | What is the frame asking us to notice? What changes perceptually while action remains slight? Why should the shot continue? | A manufactured incident every few seconds or dialogue to label the feeling. |
| Essay / authored documentary | A question or point of view develops through images, sound, evidence, memory or juxtaposition. | Who is looking? What can the material support? Does each juxtaposition open or alter an idea? | A fictional protagonist, false neutrality, invented testimony or conventional plot as proof of coherence. |
| Music-led sequence | Image and sound make or revise meaning through rhythm, contrast, repetition or silence. | Does a cut, return or visual shift change how the music is heard? What is intentionally not illustrated? | One literal shot for every lyric, or timing claims not grounded in the actual track. |
| Advertising / product film | A viewer-relevant tension connects to truthful, visible product proof and a memory or action. | Is the benefit demonstrable? Is the product causal rather than decorative? Is the CTA approved and connected? | Unsupported claims, automatic voice-over, offer, logo or CTA. |
| Short-form / social narrative | A first image, action or question earns attention; the short form resolves, reframes or deliberately leaves something. | Can the opening be understood without a generic “hook”? Does compression preserve the point? | Faster cuts or louder spectacle as a substitute for specificity. |
| Hybrid | Distinct modes are combined because their difference has meaning. | What does each mode let the audience know or feel that the other cannot? | Treating a form shift as an error or combining devices without a relation. |

These labels are lenses, not box assignments. The project may be between forms.
State uncertainty if choosing a form would pre-empt the user's decision; otherwise
make a provisional choice and explain only the consequence that matters.

## Build forward movement without forcing a beat grid

### For a causal scene or sequence

Map only the chain needed to expose causality:

`initial condition → attempt → resistance/response → changed condition → next choice`

The protagonist may have an explicit objective, but a small practical tactic,
an avoidance, an error, a withheld answer or a social ritual can also drive a
scene. Stakes may be quiet. Check that the audience can follow what happened
and why the next action became possible. Do not confuse “more events” with
escalation.

### For observation or essay

Map attention instead of fake plot:

`what is initially visible → what repeats or enters → what we now notice → what
the image/sound leaves unresolved`

A no-turn scene can be purposeful when the duration alters perception, pressure
or relation. A static image is not automatically inert; a constant stream of
events is not automatically a story.

### For music-led work

Map song sections or meaningful sonic changes only from an actual track or
supplied structure. Decide whether a phrase is answered, contradicted, withheld
from, or materially grounded by the image. Use lyric text only if provided or
otherwise authorized; do not invent quoted lyrics or imply a sync was tested.

### For product work

Separate four things: viewer tension, product fact, visible proof and desired
memory/action. The product may be an object of observation, a tool that enables
the action, a proof event, or one component of a broader story. It need not be
inserted into every scene. Keep supplied claims exact and distinguish a new
copy proposal from approved language.

## Choose the right development artifact

- **Premise / logline:** one specific situation, the change or pressure, and
  the element that makes the idea recognizable. Do not imply a complete story
  where only a question exists.
- **Pitch:** the experience, audience, form and story engine in decision-ready
  language. Include only the context needed to choose whether development
  continues.
- **Treatment:** a readable account of how the film unfolds, including visible
  behavior, key images/sounds and an ending/residue. A treatment is prose, not
  simply a table of headings or an adjective pile.
- **Beat / sequence map:** ordered events and their relationships. Each beat
  should have a reason, not just a different location. Time ranges are
  provisional unless timed against a real performance or track.
- **Scene cards:** scene purpose, place/time, entry state, action/pressure,
  shift or intentional hold, exit state, exact spoken text if locked, and
  dependencies. Add fields only when they help the handoff.
- **Screenplay:** actual scene headings and performable action/dialogue in the
  requested format. Do not stop at premise or scene summaries.
- **Coverage / critique:** material actually supplied, observed evidence,
  prioritized issue, preserve set, proposed revision and test. Do not claim
  line-by-line review of missing pages.

Scale the prework to the request. For a single scene, a short private note on
form and intended effect may be enough. For a multi-sequence feature, clarify
the engine and structure before polishing dialogue. For approval, deliver a
decision-ready pitch. For a complete screenplay, do not sell an outline as the
script.

## Originality and anti-generic development

Specificity is not ornament density. It is a decision that changes meaning:
who sees the event, what a person chooses to do, what a material allows, what
is omitted, or why this sound arrives now. Before using a stock image or phrase,
ask what the story-specific alternative would reveal. Keep a familiar device
only if it is deliberately useful, not because “cinematic” requires it.

When a direction feels generic, test one consequential change. For example,
move the scene's point of view from the person performing a task to the person
waiting for it; or remove a confirming line and see whether the action carries
enough information. Do not call a palette, lens or soundtrack change a new
story direction unless it changes what the audience understands.

Do not resolve every ambiguity. In documentary and essay work, distinguish
confirmed fact, interpretation, reconstruction and unknown. In fiction, label
inventions when they touch the user's real product, people or event. A vivid
but false detail is not a stronger draft.

## Development review

Read the requested artifact from the audience's position:

- Can I tell what kind of experience this is without an explanatory preamble?
- Is there a specific image, action, relation or sound that belongs to this
  material?
- Does movement arise from the selected form, rather than a pasted structure?
- Are repetitions, stillness and shifts intentional?
- Does the ending pay off, reframe, rest or leave open the question the piece
  actually raised?
- Has the user received the stage/output they asked for?

Repair the largest evidenced weakness. Do not turn this checklist into a visible
scoring rubric unless the user asks for one.

## Illustrative form comparison

Synthetic seed: a night attendant at a closed municipal pool notices that one
lane rope keeps drifting back toward the ladder after she has pulled it in.
This is an invented teaching premise, not a user story or a claim about a real
pool.

- **Causal option:** She tries to secure the rope before lock-up; each attempt
  shows a different mechanical or social obstacle. The next action depends on
  what the last one changed.
- **Observational option:** Keep a fixed view as she measures, waits, and
  adjusts the rope. The question is whether the changing water and repeated
  small correction hold attention; no explanation or dramatic reveal is
  promised.
- **Essay option:** A voice asks what counts as “straight” in a public place,
  while the lane rope, old pool markings and closing routine are juxtaposed.
  Each factual statement needs a source; the voice is an authored point of
  view, not neutral narration.
- **Music-led option:** A supplied repeating musical phrase coincides with the
  rope's return, then the image changes on a later repetition. This option
  requires an actual track or explicit provisional timing assumptions.

The same seed supports different experiences. Do not present all four unless
the user asked to brainstorm or compare routes.

  https://collab.sundance.org/catalog/Screenwriting-From-Outline-to-First-Draft-of-Your-Screenplay/May-2026

  https://collab.sundance.org/catalog/Screenwriting-Rewriting-Your-Feature
- BFI/Sight and Sound's Agnès Varda interview discusses first-person essay
  filmmaking and the relationship between tools and authored intent:
  https://www.bfi.org.uk/sight-and-sound/interviews/refuseniks-agnes-varda-gleaners-i
- BFI/Sight and Sound's Rithy Panh interview discusses documentary form,
  subjectivity and discovering meaning through the material:
  https://www.bfi.org.uk/sight-and-sound/interviews/memories-murder-rithy-panh-missing-picture

These sources offer curricula and distinct filmmaker perspectives. They
support a menu of craft lenses, not a universal formula, measured quality
threshold or claim that any procedure guarantees a successful film.
